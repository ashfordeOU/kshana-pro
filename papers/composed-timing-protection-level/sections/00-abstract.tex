A timing service that must ride through the loss of its cross-checking sources---a
receiver that drops from a multi-source, time-RAIM-available regime into
oscillator holdover---needs a single protection level (PL) that is valid on both
sides of that transition and, above all, does not fall at the seam. Time-RAIM,
the timing protection level, fault-tolerant time selection, and conditional
single-source holdover PLs already exist and are cited as prior art. What does
not yet exist is a single integrity-risk-budgeted timing PL that \emph{composes}
a solution-separation cross-check regime with a probabilistic holdover regime
across the handover seam \emph{without a downward, and therefore unsafe, jump}.
This paper defines that composed object and proves its two structural properties.
First, we recast the scalar timing PL as the rank-one ($H=\bm 1_N$)
specialization of MHSS-ARAIM solution separation (\Cited{} from Blanch and from
Joerger): the geometry-dilution structure of position ARAIM collapses, so the PL
is driven not by geometry but by the worst single-source-\emph{exclusion} subset's
noise overbound plus its $\utck{}$ traceability bias (\Proven{}); the $N=1$ closed
form $\PL=b+\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}\!\big(1-\tfrac{\IR/2}{1-p_{\mathrm{fault}}}\big)$
evaluates to \SI{9.83}{\nano\second} for a representative source and is
reproduced by the engine's bisection to \num{e-11} (an independent confirm).
Second---the flagship---is a \emph{modelling} insight rather than a deep limit
theorem: demanding \emph{continuity} at the cross-check--to--holdover seam is
unsafe, because the handover $\TPL$ carries fault-mode overbounds that a pure coast
lacks, so naive continuity would force the PL \emph{down} at the instant the last
cross-check is lost. The remedy is structural---we define the composed ride-through
PL as $\PL(\tau)=\max\big(\TPL_{\mathrm{handover}},\HPL(\tau)\big)$, whose
$\max(\cdot)$ guarantees no downward jump---and the resulting property,
$\PL(0^{+})\ge\PL(0^{-})$ (\Proven{}), is what we \emph{name}
lower-semicontinuity and non-decrease across handover. This is deliberately
\emph{not} a continuity claim and \emph{not} $\HPL(0)=\TPL_{\mathrm{handover}}$;
seeding the coast with the disciplining filter's handover covariance $\Pzero$ is a
safety requirement, not an option (an un-seeded PL drops). We turn the known impossibility of a finite deterministic
worst-case holdover into a positive law-of-the-iterated-logarithm statement: the
almost-sure Brownian envelope $\sqrt{2Dt\ln\ln t}$ diverges (no worst-case bound
exists), yet the integrity-risk-allocated $\HPL$ dominates that a.s.\ envelope as
$\IR\to0$ (\Proven{}, asymptotic only---it certifies neither a finite-$t$ value
nor the \num{e-7} quantile). Exactly one quantitative claim is externally
\Validated{}: the holdover leg's running-max envelope \emph{overbound-covers} a
real BIPM Circular-T $[\utck{}-\mathrm{UTC(USNO)}]$ series (872 points, MJD
56074--60429) with zero piercing on a disjoint late segment for every multi-year
lag $\tau\ge\SI{90}{\day}$; the short-lag $\tau=\SI{30}{\day}$ row is pierced and
is disclosed as a \Modelled{} boundary. The composition, the heterogeneous
budget, and every scenario magnitude are \Proven{} algebra and \Modelled{}
scenarios---never \Validated{}. Baweja's conditional TPL is recovered as the
$N=1$, single-transition special case this generalizes. The result is a small,
honestly bounded contribution: the seam nobody owns, made a lower-semicontinuous
object with one real-data coverage anchor.
