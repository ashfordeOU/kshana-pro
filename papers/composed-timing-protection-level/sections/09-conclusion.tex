\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A timing user that must survive the loss of its cross-checking sources needs a
protection level that spans the transition into holdover and, above all, does not
fall at the seam. This paper defines that composed timing protection level,
$\PL(\tau)=\max(\TPL_{\mathrm{handover}},\HPL(\tau))$, and establishes its one
load-bearing structural property: it is lower-semicontinuous and non-decreasing
across handover, $\PL(0^{+})\ge\PL(0^{-})$, so it never jumps downward at the
instant the last independent check is lost. This is deliberately not a continuity
claim---the handover TPL carries fault-mode overbounds a pure coast lacks, so
continuity would force an unsafe downward jump---and seeding the coast with the
disciplining filter's handover covariance $\Pzero$ is a proved safety requirement,
not an option.

The supporting results are honestly bounded. The scalar timing TPL is the rank-one
($H=\bm 1_N$) specialization of MHSS-ARAIM solution separation, in which the
geometry-dilution structure collapses and the PL becomes driven by the worst
single-source-exclusion subset's bias overbound rather than by geometry; its $N=1$
closed form evaluates to \SI{9.83}{\nano\second} and is reproduced by the engine's
general solver to below \num{e-11}. The holdover leg is a $\Pzero$-seeded,
integrity-risk-allocated envelope with the correct phase-error exponents
$\tau,\tau^{3},\tau^{5}$, a running-max multiplier $\Kf(\IR/2)$ that is exact for
the white-FM leg and conservative for the higher legs, and a bounded flicker
inflation. The law of the iterated logarithm turns the known impossibility of a
finite worst-case holdover into a positive statement: no such bound exists, yet
the allocated envelope has the right growth order to overbound the almost-sure
path as the integrity risk tightens---asymptotically, certifying neither a
finite-$t$ value nor the deep tail. And exactly one claim is externally
\Validated{}: the holdover leg's running-max envelope overbound-covers a real BIPM
$[\utck{}-\mathrm{UTC(USNO)}]$ series on a disjoint segment for every multi-year
lag $\tau\ge\SI{90}{\day}$, with the short-lag boundary disclosed as \Modelled{}.
Baweja's conditional TPL is recovered as the $N=1$ special case this generalizes.

The composed PL is intended, not asserted, to be a reference object---status of
that kind is conferred only by external adoption. It is the method half of a
planned arc whose companion papers add the heterogeneous $\utck{}$-traceable
budget, the common-mode correction, the adversarial protection level and
authenticated protocol, and an open timing-integrity benchmark; the benchmark is
the yardstick meant to be cited alongside this method. What this paper contributes
on its own is small and, we hope, durable: the seam nobody owned, made a
lower-semicontinuous object, with one real-data coverage anchor and its scope
stated in full. Every number here is reproducible from the open engine by a single
command.
