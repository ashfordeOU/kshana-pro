\label{sec:availability}
The benchmark is part of the open-source \code{kshana} engine
\citep{baweja2026kshana} (\url{https://github.com/ashfordeOU/kshana}; AGPL-3.0-only).
The scoring method of Section~\ref{sec:method}---the four-region epoch
classification \eqref{eq:classify} and the integrity-coverage verdict
\eqref{eq:covok}---is in \code{src/benchmark/stanford.rs} and
\code{src/benchmark/coverage.rs}; the fault menu of Section~\ref{sec:faultmenu},
with its \Cited{} detectability partition, is in \code{src/benchmark/faults.rs};
and the verdict algebra of Section~\ref{sec:verdict}, including the impossibility
invariant of Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}, is in \code{src/benchmark/scorecard.rs}.
No result is hard-coded in a print statement: the scorecards are computed by the
committed scorer over the committed generators.

The demonstration of Section~\ref{sec:demo}---the reference composed protection
level (\SI{27.0}{\nano\second} at handover, \SI{264.8}{\nano\second} at
\SI{24}{\hour}) and the broken \SI{5}{\nano\second} monitor, Tables~\ref{tab:refcard}
and~\ref{tab:brokencard}---is regenerated by the committed example
\begin{quote}
  \code{cargo run -{}-example tib\_scorecard}
\end{quote}
which builds the reference monitor from the three representative sources, evaluates
$\PL(\tau)=\max(\TPL_{\mathrm{handover}},\mathrm{HPL}(\tau))$ over the one-day
coast, runs both monitors through the menu at $\AL=\SI{100}{\nano\second}$ and
$\IR=\num{e-3}$, prints both scorecards, and asserts the honesty properties live:
that the reference absorbs the sub-floor undetectables, that the broken monitor
fails to and is flagged at least as heavily, and that no undetectable scenario ever
receives a coverage (detection-flavoured) verdict.

The \Proven{} invariant and the scorer arithmetic are checked by tests. The
verdict-algebra impossibility (Theorem~\ref{thm:invariant}) is asserted by the unit
tests in \code{src/benchmark/scorecard.rs} (an undetectable scenario never yields a
coverage verdict, for any protection level) and enforced on the prose by
\code{tests/tib\_honesty\_gates.rs} (no source line naming an undetectable fault
may assert detection; forbidden overclaim phrases and stray attribution tokens
fail the build; the Stanford, WAAS, and Mizrahi (RFC~7384) method citations must be
present; a moved or renamed benchmark source fails the gate loudly rather than being
silently skipped).
The scorer's \code{InternalConsistency} cross-check of Section~\ref{sec:oracle} is
reproduced by
\begin{quote}
  \code{python3 scripts/gen\_tib\_scorer\_reference.py}\\
  \code{cargo test -{}-test tib\_scorer\_reference}
\end{quote}
The \code{numpy} script classifies a fixed synthetic $(e,\PL,\AL)$ sample set by
the four-region rule and writes \code{tests/fixtures/tib/reference.json} (the region
counts $n=80$, $40$/$20$/$10$/$10$ across \Nominal{}/\MI{}/\HMI{}/\Unavail{}, the
rates, and $\mathrm{coverage\_ok}$); the Rust test reconstructs the counts
independently and fails the build on any mismatch to \num{e-12}. The fixture's
\code{NOTICE.md} records the honesty posture explicitly: it is \emph{not} an
external dataset and \emph{not} a validated accuracy oracle---the benchmark is
honesty-immune, so only the counting is cross-checked, and no row of this work is
\Validated{}. In the engine's verification matrix the four benchmark rows are each
\code{Modelled} with an \code{InternalConsistency} oracle; none is \code{Validated}
and none carries an \code{ExternalDataset} accuracy oracle. A productized
operational timing-integrity capability, as opposed to the open reference substrate
that reproduces this paper's numbers, is a commercial capability of
\code{kshana-pro} and is not part of this open release.
