\section{Introduction}

Lunar PNT is moving from concept to procurement. ESA's Moonlight initiative will
field a lunar communications and navigation service (LCNS) from a small
constellation; a NovaMoon-class lunar geodetic-timing-differential station
concept brings multi-technique geodesy (VLBI, ranging), joint orbit-and-clock
determination, lunar reference-frame realisation, lunar timekeeping, and
differential corrections to the surface; LunaNet and the IOAG are defining
cross-agency interoperability; and a Coordinated Lunar Time has been called for,
with its relativistic rate (${\sim}57$~$\mu$s/day faster than
terrestrial time) now a standards question \citep{petit2010iers}. Every one of
these rests on the same engineering core: \textbf{determining the orbits and
clocks of a lunar PNT infrastructure and tying it into a terrestrial or inertial
reference frame.}

A first-order design question for that core is \emph{observability}: given a set
of measurement types, which states (positions, clocks, frame parameters) are
well determined, and which are not? The answer drives architecture --- whether a
lunar nav service needs Earth-baseline geodetic ties, how many, and how precise.
Today this question is answered in private, one-off studies whose assumptions are
neither shared nor reproducible. There is no open, citable tool in which a lunar
PNT network's observability can be regenerated from a committed scenario, with
every figure of merit labelled as externally validated or modelled --- the gap
the kshana program has documented for terrestrial and deep-space PNT
\citep{baweja2026kshana}, here for the lunar case
\citep{iiyama2023lupnt,fernandezprades2018reproducibility}.

We contribute (i) an open lunar PNT toolkit (seven modules in the \code{kshana}
engine) and (ii) one clean, falsifiable observability result obtained with it:
\textbf{Earth-baseline VLBI restores the observability of a lunar surface
station's absolute 3-D position in joint orbit-and-clock determination, and does
so through information rather than observation count, selectively, and only above
a minimum baseline count.}
