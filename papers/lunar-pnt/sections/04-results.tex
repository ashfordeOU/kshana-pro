\section{Results}

\subsection{VLBI restores absolute station observability}

In the default scenario (3 satellites, 6 Earth stations, seed 42), adding
Earth-baseline VLBI reduces the recovered lunar surface station 3-D position
error from \textbf{2183~m (range-only) to 3.55~m}, a \textbf{615$\times$}
sharpening; satellite position RMS falls from 717~m to 2.20~m. The fit converges
in two iterations with a weighted RMS residual of 0.05 (40 observations, 16
parameters). Figure~\ref{fig:headline}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig1_headline}
  \caption{With vs without Earth-baseline VLBI in the default scenario
    (3 satellites, 6 Earth stations, seed 42). Left: recovered position errors
    --- adding VLBI sharpens the lunar surface station's absolute 3-D error from
    2183~m (range-only) to 3.55~m ($615\times$) and satellite position RMS from
    717~m to 2.20~m. Right: clocks --- the satellite clock RMS improves by
    ${\sim}569\times$ while the station clock, already pinned by the clock-sync
    pseudo-observable, is unchanged (ratio 1.005). \textbf{Modelled}
    (internal-consistency oracle).}
  \label{fig:headline}
\end{figure}

That single seed is favourable. Across a \textbf{200-seed} truth/noise ensemble
the median recovered station error with VLBI is \textbf{13.4~m} (IQR
5.9--23.0~m) versus a range-only median of ${\sim}793$~m --- a \textbf{median
improvement factor of 91$\times$} (IQR 38--208$\times$;
Figure~\ref{fig:seed_mc}). We report the median as the headline result; the
default-scenario seed sits in the upper tail. In a small fraction of seeds the
factor is near unity (minimum 0.91 over 200 seeds): VLBI is not guaranteed to
help on every draw, and we say so.

\subsection{Information, not observation count}

Holding the observation set fixed and degrading the VLBI delay $\sigma$ from
$10^{-12}$~s to $10^{-7}$~s drives the median station error overall from
\textbf{1.6~m to ${\sim}391$~m}, approaching the range-only median of
${\sim}793$~m (Figure~\ref{fig:sigma_vlbi}). The extra observations are present
throughout; only their information content changes. This is the decisive
control: the improvement is VLBI \textbf{information}, not the fifteen additional
observations. The default $\sigma$ (${\sim}3$~mm delay precision, representative
of geodetic VLBI) sits near the informative end of the curve.

\subsection{The benefit is selective}

VLBI sharpens \emph{absolute} states. In the default scenario the satellite
clock RMS improves by ${\sim}569\times$ ($4.1\times10^{-6}$~s $\rightarrow$
$7.3\times10^{-9}$~s) alongside the position gains --- but the \textbf{station
clock error is unchanged} ($2.882\times10^{-10}$~s $\rightarrow$
$2.868\times10^{-10}$~s, ratio 1.005), because the clock-sync pseudo-observable
already pins it (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}, right). VLBI is not a universal
improver; it supplies exactly the absolute-geometry information the lunar-local
set lacks, and nothing the lunar-local set already provides.

\subsection{A minimum baseline count is required}

Sweeping the number of Earth stations shows the joint fit is \textbf{rank-deficient
with one or two stations} (no finite solution) and becomes well-posed at
\textbf{three}, after which the median station error decreases with more baselines
and diminishing returns (${\sim}30$~m at 3 stations to ${\sim}10$~m at 10;
Figure~\ref{fig:n_earth}). A geodetic VLBI tie to a lunar PNT network therefore
has a minimum-infrastructure threshold, not merely a nice-to-have margin.

\subsection{Cross-suite sanity}

The same engine produces physically sensible numbers across the lunar chain
(Figure~\ref{fig:cross_suite}): Coordinated Lunar Time at
\textbf{57.04~$\mu$s/day}, inside the published
56--59~$\mu$s/day band, decomposed into a
$+57.50$~$\mu$s/day self-potential term and a
$-0.46$~$\mu$s/day kinetic term; a lunar-distance VLBI
\textbf{near-field correction of 58.9~$\mu$s} on a 10\,727~km
baseline, with the far-field limit reproducing the engine's independent
$\Delta$-DOR delay to machine precision (unit-test oracle); and differential PNT
decorrelation reducing a 24.5~m standalone user error to \textbf{0.008~m at a
50~km baseline} ($3186\times$), growing with baseline as expected.
