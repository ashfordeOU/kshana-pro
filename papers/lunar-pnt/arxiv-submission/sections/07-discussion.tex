\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

For anyone designing a NovaMoon-class reference station or a Moonlight/LCNS
architecture, the finding is a concrete and reproducible reason the Earth-baseline
geodetic tie matters. Lunar-local measurements fix the cluster's internal
geometry on their own, but they leave its absolute placement weakly observable,
and a few precise Earth VLBI baselines close that gap, above a three-station
threshold and through information rather than count.

That gives a sizing rule straight from the information matrix, not a rule of thumb.
The floor is non-negotiable: at least three non-collinear Earth stations, because
below three the joint network is rank-deficient and the absolute-position bound is
infinite---verify it on the datum defect ($\dim\ker M = 0$), not on a merely finite
residual. Above the floor the Cram\'er--Rao bound is the design curve: the default
six-station tie reaches \SI{20.1}{\meter} on the station's absolute position and
ten stations reach \SI{13.7}{\meter}, with diminishing returns set by the
plane-of-sky baseline geometry. For a safety-of-life absolute datum the criterion
to optimise is E-optimality---lift the worst-observed axis, the very one the
Earth baseline exists to fix---rather than the average-variance or
ellipsoid-volume criteria that a well-conditioned network would let you use
interchangeably; \code{fim}'s \code{best\_design} selects among candidate
geometries on whichever criterion the mission sets. Because the estimator is
efficient (\S\ref{sec:theory}), the bound is what you will actually get, so the
curve can be used for sizing directly.

The real programme has already reached the same conclusion. ESA's NovaMoon
station, adopted at CM25, carries an Earth-baseline VLBI transmitter as a core
element \citep{molli2026novamoon}, which is the design choice this study points
to. The point generalises beyond ESA: because LCNS, NASA's LCRNS and JAXA's LNSS
share the Augmented Forward Signal under LNIS~v5
\citep{nasa2025lnis,nasa2025afs}, the weak-absolute-observability gap belongs to
the common architecture, not to any one provider. LNIS~v5 also still leaves the
binding lunar frame and time realisation to a later document, so an open,
honestly Modelled, LNIS-vocabulary-aligned candidate that a mission team can run
today is useful right now rather than eventually. The toolkit lets that team
reproduce the trade-off and vary it before any hardware exists, with the honesty
labels intact. To be clear, we are claiming that the architectural rationale is
corroborated, not that kshana's numbers are validated (see Limitations).
