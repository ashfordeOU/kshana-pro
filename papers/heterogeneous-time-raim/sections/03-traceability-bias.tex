\section{The $\utck{}$ traceability-bias overbound}
\label{sec:trace}

The companion paper carried each source's bias $b_i$ as a given input and deferred
its construction. This section builds it, from published metrology, as a one-sided
bounded overbound at the allocated integrity tail. The raw material---Type-B
expanded uncertainties and per-source delay decompositions---is \Cited{}; the
reconstruction into an integrity-tail bias term is the \Proven{} contribution, and
its honest scope is stated as sharply as the construction.

\subsection{From a Type-B expanded uncertainty to an integrity-tail bias}
\label{sec:overbound}

A metrology laboratory publishes a source's traceability to $\utck{}$ as a Type-B
\emph{expanded} uncertainty $U_i$ at a stated coverage factor $k_{\mathrm{cov}}$
(conventionally $k_{\mathrm{cov}}=2$, nominally $95\%$), following the GUM
\citep{jcgm100}. The standard uncertainty is recovered as
$u_i=U_i/k_{\mathrm{cov}}$. An integrity budget, however, is not written at $95\%$;
it is written at an allocated per-source integrity tail. We therefore reconstruct
the published expanded uncertainty into a bias overbound at that tail,
\begin{equation}
  b_i \;=\; \frac{U_i}{k_{\mathrm{cov}}}\,
            \Phi^{-1}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{\mathrm{tail}}{2}\Big)
            \;+\; \mathrm{ageing}_i,
  \label{eq:overbound}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi^{-1}$ is the standard-normal inverse CDF, $\mathrm{tail}$ is the
per-source integrity tail allocated to the traceability term (two-sided), and
$\mathrm{ageing}_i$ is a deterministic drift-of-bias allowance over the exposure
interval (for the local oscillator this is the standard clock-model ageing
\citep{zucca2005}; for a metrology-steered source it is the between-calibration
drift of the published offset). Two modelling commitments make~\eqref{eq:overbound}
honest rather than a sleight of hand.

\begin{remark}[This is a category-consistent reconstruction, not a $\sigma$
inflation]
\label{rem:category}
The companion paper warned specifically against inflating a Type-B $k=2$
metrological uncertainty into a deep-tail \emph{Gaussian $\sigma$}, which is a
category error: a systematic offset is not a noise process, and treating it as one
double-counts it in the solution-separation variance. Equation~\eqref{eq:overbound}
does the opposite. It first \emph{deflates} the published expanded uncertainty back
to the standard uncertainty $u_i=U_i/k_{\mathrm{cov}}$, then re-expresses that
standard uncertainty as a \emph{bounded bias} $b_i$ at the integrity tail, carried
structurally separate from the Gaussian $\sigma_i$ in the ARAIM manner. The factor
$\Phi^{-1}(1-\mathrm{tail}/2)$ carries an explicit, load-bearing overbounding
assumption, which we own rather than disclaim: $b_i=u_i\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\mathrm{tail}/2)$
bounds $|X|\le b_i$ at probability $1-\mathrm{tail}$ only under a \emph{zero-mean
Gaussian CDF-overbound} of the traceability-bias error, in the DeCleene-style
paired-overbounding sense. That Gaussian overbound is the operative modelling
assumption of the reconstruction, not an incidental convenience: for a general
zero-mean error of standard deviation $u_i$ a distribution-free (Chebyshev) bound at
the same coverage is weaker by orders of magnitude (at a per-side $\num{e-7}$ tail,
roughly $\Phi^{-1}(1-\num{e-7})^{-2}\!\approx\!4\%$ versus the Gaussian
$2\times\num{e-7}$), so the Gaussian-overbound assumption is precisely what buys the
stated tail, and we state it as such. Under that overbound the tail scaling is exact;
the deep tail itself remains \Modelled{} (Section~\ref{sec:tracescope}), because no
monthly metrological series can validate a $\num{e-7}$ quantile. Two further points
keep the \emph{one-sided} language honest. The overbound is \emph{symmetric}: it
bounds $|X|\le b_i$ with two-sided probability $1-\mathrm{tail}$, which is exactly why
the two-sided quantile $\Phi^{-1}(1-\mathrm{tail}/2)$ is the correct multiplier. We
call $b_i$ ``one-sided'' only in the sense that it enters the protection level as a
single $\pm b_i$ worst-case displacement of a bounded systematic offset, not that a
single tail is bounded.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Honest scope: the deep tail is \Modelled{}}
\label{sec:tracescope}

The load-bearing honesty statement of this section is about the tail. A monthly
metrological bulletin---Circular-T is published at five-day cadence, order
$10^2$--$10^3$ points per source-year---cannot \emph{validate} a deep integrity
quantile such as a two-sided $\mathrm{tail}=\num{2e-7}$ (per-side $\num{e-7}$); there
is no sample support at that
probability, and no amount of bookkeeping changes that. We therefore label the
deep-tail evaluation of~\eqref{eq:overbound} \Modelled{}: it is the
integrity-risk-allocated extrapolation of a \Cited{} $95\%$ metrological input, and
it inherits exactly the confidence of that extrapolation and no more. The published
magnitudes $U_i$ that seed~\eqref{eq:overbound}---GNSS time-transfer delay
decompositions \citep{defraigne2015}, the UTC realization budget
\citep{panfilo2019}, and the Circular-T $[\utck{}-\utck{}]$ offsets
\citep{bipmcirculart}---are \Cited{}. The reconstruction map~\eqref{eq:overbound} is
\Proven{} algebra. The specific tail, and any specific $b_i$ read as an operational
tolerance, are \Modelled{}. We claim no \Validated{} status for any $b_i$; the
Circular-T series that sizes them is P1's oracle, a \Cited{} input here, and is not
re-validated (Section~\ref{sec:datasetnote}).
