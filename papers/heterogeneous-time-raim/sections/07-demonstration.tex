\section{Demonstration}
\label{sec:demo}

We exercise both constructions end to end on a single heterogeneous scenario, by
the committed example \code{examples/hetero\_gls\_demo.rs}. Every number in this
section is printed by that example; none is transcribed by hand into the code, and
all are \Modelled{}---representative magnitudes chosen to make the algebra visible,
not operational tolerances and carrying no certification implication. There is no
accuracy claim in this section.

\subsection{Scenario}
\label{sec:demoscenario}

The scenario is the five-source set of~\eqref{eq:sourceset}: a GNSS-PPP source
(index $0$), an eLoran/R-Mode source (index $1$), a PTP/White-Rabbit source
(index $2$), a LEO-STL source (index $3$), and a local oscillator (index $4$). The
shared-realizer structure is the load-bearing choice: \emph{sources $0$ and $1$
both trace $\utc{}(\mathrm{USNO})$}, so their traceability biases are coupled by a
positive off-diagonal in $\Sib{}$ (a two-source realizer group with correlation
$\rho$), while sources $2$--$4$ are independent. Per-source noise overbounds
$\sigma_i$, bias overbounds $b_i$ from~\eqref{eq:overbound}, and fault priors
$p_{\mathrm{fault},i}$ are representative nanosecond-scale values set in the
example; we report the fused outputs rather than assert the inputs as tolerances.

\subsection{R2: correlated bias exceeds the naive independent fusion}
\label{sec:demor2}

Fusing the biases with the shared-realizer coupling of~\eqref{eq:corrgeqindep}
gives a correlated fused bias of
\begin{equation}
  \text{correlated fused bias} \;=\; \SI{7.788}{\nano\second},
  \qquad
  \text{naive independent (RSS)} \;=\; \SI{6.681}{\nano\second},
  \label{eq:demor2}
\end{equation}
an understatement of $\Delta=\SI{1.107}{\nano\second}$ that a variance- or RSS-style
allocation ignoring the shared $\utc{}(\mathrm{USNO})$ realizer would incur. This is
the concrete form of Theorem~\ref{thm:corr}: correlated $\ge$ independent, with the
gap living entirely in the source-$0$/source-$1$ cross term. We stress the reading
demanded by caveat~I3 (Remark~\ref{rem:i3}): the $\SI{1.107}{\nano\second}$ is the
error of the \emph{naive independent} treatment, not a margin missing from the
companion protection level---the companion's \emph{linear} bias fusion is at or
above the $\SI{7.788}{\nano\second}$ correlated value, so this demonstration warns
against a variance-style allocation and does not harden P1.

\subsection{R3: the common-mode statistic responds to a shift separation cannot see}
\label{sec:demor3}

Running solution separation on the whitened residual~\eqref{eq:whiten} and
evaluating the common-mode statistic~\eqref{eq:cmstat} on a pre-fit residual against
an independent anchor gives
\begin{equation}
  \scm{}^{\text{null}} \;=\; 0.01,
  \qquad
  \scm{}^{\text{common shift}} \;=\; 0.68 ,
  \label{eq:demor3}
\end{equation}
so the statistic rises by nearly two orders of magnitude in response to an
all-source shift that solution separation, forming contrasts orthogonal to
$\bmone{}$, reports as nominal. We state the honest limit explicitly: $0.68$ is
\emph{below} the $\chi^2_1$ $95\%$ threshold $3.841$, so this run demonstrates that
the statistic \emph{responds} to the common-mode fault, not that it trips a $95\%$
test at this magnitude; a larger shift, or a deeper integrity-allocated threshold,
would be needed to trip. It is a response demonstration, not a detection claim.

\subsection{R3: the undetectable ceiling is finite}
\label{sec:democeiling}

Finally we evaluate the undetectable ceiling~\eqref{eq:ceiling} along the modelled
common-coupling axis. It is
\begin{equation}
  \xi^\star \;=\; \min\big(\alpha_{\mathrm{ss}},\alpha_{\mathrm{cm}}\big) \;=\; 2.371
  \quad\text{(normalized units)},
  \label{eq:democeiling}
\end{equation}
a \emph{finite} value, exactly as Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} requires under a
positive-definite whitening: the modelled common axis is not a joint blind
direction of both detectors, so the residual undetectable magnitude is bounded.
Per caveat~I1 (Remark~\ref{rem:i1}), $2.371$ is a large-but-finite ceiling and,
because $\alpha_{\mathrm{ss}}$ is the idealized parity detector, a conservative
\emph{lower} bound on the true undetectable magnitude, not an infinite blind spot.

\subsection{What the demonstration shows, and what it does not}
\label{sec:demoscope}

The run shows three things: a shared realizer makes correlated bias fusion strictly
exceed the naive independent fusion (the R2 warning); the common-mode statistic
responds to a shift that solution separation cannot see (the R3 blindness); and the
undetectable ceiling along the modelled common axis is finite (the R3 blind spot,
large but bounded). It shows \emph{none} of the following: it is not an accuracy
claim, not a certification, not evidence that $\SI{7.788}{\nano\second}$ or
$2.371$ is an operational tolerance, and---because the scenario is a handful of
sources at one epoch---not a resolution of any deep-tail integrity quantile. The
magnitudes are \Modelled{} illustration of \Proven{} algebra over \Cited{}
metrology, and the demonstration adds no \Validated{} tag.
