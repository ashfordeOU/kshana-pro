\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We generalized the companion scalar timing protection level
\citep{baweja2026cti} in two orthogonal directions and stated the honest limits of
both. First, a heterogeneous, source-agnostic integrity budget in which each
source's $\utck{}$ traceability bias is reconstructed from a published Type-B
expanded uncertainty into an integrity-tail overbound, and sources sharing a
realizer are coupled by a positive-semidefinite bias cross-covariance; we proved
that a variance-style fusion of correlated biases is at least the naive independent
fusion, and we showed---this is caveat~I3---that the result does \emph{not} harden
the companion protection level, whose linear bias fusion already dominates, but
instead motivates that linear treatment. Second, a generalized-least-squares
common-mode layer that whitens the correlated residual and adds a $\chi^2_1$
consistency statistic for the shift solution separation structurally cannot see;
we showed---caveat~I2---that the statistic needs an independent anchor and vanishes
identically on the post-fit residual, so common mode is unobservable from the fused
sources alone, and---caveat~I1---that the residual undetectable blind spot is large
but \emph{finite} under a positive-definite whitening, with our ceiling a
conservative lower bound. The contributions are \Proven{} algebra over \Cited{}
metrology, exercised on \Modelled{} scenarios; this paper earns \emph{no} new
externally \Validated{} tag, and the one \Validated{} anchor in the arc---the
companion's holdover overbound coverage against the BIPM Circular-T series---enters
here only as a \Cited{} input. The composed object is small and, we hope, useful:
the source-agnostic time-RAIM budget and the common-mode layer that nobody had
written down together, delivered with the blind spot named rather than hidden.
