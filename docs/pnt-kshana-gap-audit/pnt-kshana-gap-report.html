P1-lunar-signal-security The Lunar Navigation Signal Is Unauthenticated: Sizing the Spoofing and Jamming Attack Surface of the LunaNet Augmented Forward Signal

The LunaNet Augmented Forward Signal (AFS) that NASA/ESA/JAXA will broadcast carries no signal-level authentication. A closed-form link budget shows the AFS reaches the lunar surface at ~-140.6 dBW, ~15.6 dB (factor ~36, 12-18 dB band) below terrestrial GPS at -125 dBW, so jamming/spoofing margins are proportionally thinner. Under a 3 dB capture / 30 dB denial J/S criterion a surface spoofer needs only 0.022 mW at 1 km and 0.222 W at 100 km; a surface attacker is bounded by the airless horizon (2 m mast -> 5 km, 1000 m ridge -> 61 km) but a single ~40 W orbital transmitter illuminates the whole visible hemisphere, and no lunar monitoring network exists. The paper sizes a three-layer defense (OSNMA-style message authentication, cross-sensor IMU/TRN integrity, sparse known-location monitors), argues the fix requires no new physics, and marks every result Validated (closed-form radiometry) or Modelled (representative service geometry).

P2-surface-beacon-dop The Cost of Lunar South-Polar Geometry, and Surface Beacons as the Efficient Fix: A Dilution-of-Precision Analysis

Orbit-only ELFO lunar constellations pay a steep satellite-count cost for south-polar geometry because visible satellites cluster into a small overhead solid angle: median GDOP stays ~16-21 at the planned 4-6 satellites and only crosses 6 near N~12. Three surface ranging beacons (lunar pseudolites) on 2 km elevated terrain ringing a -80 deg user supply the missing near-horizon horizontal diversity, cutting median GDOP from 16.2 to 1.6 (factor ~10, better than a 24-satellite fleet) at a fraction of the cost. The airless-body geometric horizon makes beacon siting a genuine placement optimization. The GDOP engine is Validated; the constellation and beacon scenario are Modelled.

P3-lunar-time-frame-limited Realizing Coordinated Lunar Time at a User Is Frame-Limited, Not Clock-Limited: A Systems Error Budget

Given a defined Coordinated Lunar Time (TCL/TL), the binding constraint on a user realizing it to the ~1 ns level positioning needs is NOT the atomic clock. Placing clock stability, Earth-Moon time-transfer link floors, and lunar frame/ephemeris knowledge on one axis (timing error at tau=1 day, quadrature-summed), the terms that meet 1 ns are a ground optical master clock (0.009 ns), optical two-way transfer (0.035 ns), post-processed frame (0.90 ns) and a space PHM (0.99 ns); the terms that MISS are real-time frame/ephemeris knowledge (~50 ns, the ~15 m real-time position budget as light time) and a SWaP-limited 500 g miniRAFS (~151 ns/day). Roadmap implication: the highest-leverage investment is real-time frame/ephemeris prediction (an estimation problem), not further clock improvement.

P4-realtime-frame-eop The lunar real-time frame gap is an Earth-orientation prediction problem: decomposing the 15 m to sub-meter budget

The lumped lunar real-time frame budget (~15 m real-time vs ~0.27 m post-processed, worth ~50 ns) is largely Earth-orientation (UT1-UTC) prediction error amplified by the Earth-Moon lever arm (delta_r = D_EM * omega_earth * delta_UT1). Inverting the exact lever arm on the two published lunar budgets recovers IERS final (~0.010 ms) and multi-day-predicted (~0.5 ms, ~5-day) UT1 accuracies, so the real-time gap is an EOP-prediction gap. The prescribed lever is hardware-free: shortening the EOP prediction horizon from ~5 days to ~1 day buys ~5x in real-time lunar position knowledge from the ground segment. The lever arm is Validated (exact geometry); the error-growth-vs-horizon curve is a two-anchor power law (Modelled) explicitly flagged for replacement by real IERS Bulletin-A prediction statistics.

P5 Precision Without Continuity: Why Lunar Optical PNT Needs an RF Integrity Backstop, and a Hybrid Handoff Framework

Optical Earth-Moon links are 100-1000x more precise than RF (mm/ps vs m/ns) but weather- and pointing-limited: a single optical ground station is available ~53% of the time and even a 5-6 station diversity network saturates near 95-96%, short of RF's >99.5%. Because a PNT service demands continuity and integrity, not just precision, an optical-only architecture is not a service. The resolution and open problem is a hybrid: RF provides always-on continuity plus an independent cross-modality integrity channel while optical provides precision, bound by a discontinuity-free receiver handoff and a joint availability/precision/integrity trade specification. The paper is explicit that the link/availability numbers are Modelled and that the hybrid integrity framing is the (incremental) contribution.

P6 Cislunar Positioning Is an Observability Problem, Not a Geometry Problem: Why Snapshot GDOP Does Not Apply to DRO and Libration Constellations

Snapshot GDOP is structurally inapplicable to cislunar full-state orbit determination: for a CR3BP DRO constellation the instantaneous inter-satellite ranging Jacobian is rank-deficient (rank 1 of 4, velocity unobservable) so GDOP is undefined, and full-state observability accrues only over a ~1 hour tracking arc as CR3BP dynamics propagate position information into velocity through the state-transition matrix; the correct figure of merit is a functional of the observability Gramian (rank, minimum eigenvalue, condition number) along the orbit rather than snapshot GDOP.
