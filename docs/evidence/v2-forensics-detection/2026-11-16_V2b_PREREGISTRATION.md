## 1. Diagnosis of the miss (from the published results only)

* **The miss:** B3 cross-attribution 4 / 24 (bar <= 2 / 24). All four are jamming cases also
  indicated as spoofing: S04, S08, S32, S40, with injected C/N0 drops of 24.9, 27.9, 25.6 and
  25.5 dB. Every jamming case with a drop of 29.5 dB or more (7 cases) and the one at 20.6 dB was
  attributed to jamming alone. The spoofing cases, clean runs and confounders were all right
  (B1s 12/12, B2 0/24).
* **What the v1 rule does.** An episode is spoofing-like when at least 20 % of its epochs carry a
  signal-shape alarm (`cn0-spread`, `cn0-rise`) or a state alarm (`position-jump`, `raim`,
  `clock`, `kinematic`, `heading-course`, `speed-log`, `sea-level`, `time-consistency`, `osnma`),
  counted whether or not a jamming alarm fires in the same epoch. `cn0-spread` is the open
  monitor for a **collapse** of the C/N0 spread across satellites (counterfeit signals arrive at
  similar power).
* **Mechanism (unverified: `results.json` records the verdicts, not which monitors fired).** A
  moderate drop (about 24 to 28 dB, with 6 dB per-satellite spread) leaves some satellites
  tracked near the tracking floor while the weaker ones are lost, so the surviving C/N0 values
  bunch together: the spread collapses as it would under spoofing, but at **low** power. A deeper
  drop removes nearly every satellite (no spread is formed); a shallower one leaves the spread
  intact. The weakened solution can also trip position-domain state monitors through noise.
  Either path makes a jamming episode count as spoofing-like. This is a band of drops, which
  matches the four cases.

## 2. The method (fixed now; `kshana-pro/incident-forensics/v2`)

`kshana-pro/incident-forensics/v1` (`MethodParams::V1`) unchanged, except how an epoch counts
toward the spoofing-like share of an episode (`spoof_n` in `src/forensics/detect.rs`):

* **R1, power consistency of signal-shape evidence.** A `cn0-spread` or `cn0-rise` alarm counts
  only in an epoch where no jamming-family monitor (`cn0-drop`, `agc`, `jam-ind`,
  `loss-of-lock`, `solve-failure`) fires **and** the median C/N0 of the tracked satellites is not
  more than **6.0 dB** below the baseline median. Reason: a spoofer that takes over the
  receiver's tracking does so with counterfeit signals at or above the authentic power; a jammer
  lowers every satellite's C/N0. A spread collapse at a lowered median is the tracking floor,
  not a counterfeit constellation. (6 dB is fixed a priori, well below V2's smallest injected
  drop of 20 dB and, we expect, well above the epoch-to-epoch movement of the median C/N0 of an
  unjammed receiver (unverified); it is not tuned on any set.)
* **R2, noise-driven state evidence under jamming.** In an epoch where a jamming-family monitor
  fires, the position-domain state monitors (`position-jump`, `raim`, `clock`, `kinematic`,
  `heading-course`, `speed-log`) do not count toward `spoof_n`. `time-consistency`, `osnma` and
  `sea-level` count in every epoch. Reason: with fewer and weaker satellites the solution's noise
  and its consistency statistics grow with no counterfeit signal; a jammer removes data but
  cannot make authentication fail or the stated time step inconsistently.
* Everything else (the 0.2 shares, categories, breadth and obstruction tests, confidence points,
  onset bounds, AIS and ADS-B paths) is v1's. The method id in every report becomes `.../v2`.

Known cost, stated now: an attack that spoofs **only while** it also jams would lose
spoofing-like epochs under R2. The synthetic set below has no such class; the public-event part
may (V2P reports it).

receiver case, which monitors counted toward each family (so the mechanism in section 1 can be
confirmed or refuted). Published whatever the outcome; E1 stays unclaimed unless V2b and V2P
both pass.
