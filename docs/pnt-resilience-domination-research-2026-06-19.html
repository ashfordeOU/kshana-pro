The target SciOpen URL (JGGS 2023, "Development of Comprehensive PNT and Resilient PNT", Yang Yuanxi) is a metadata-only landing page (no body text), so I synthesised from the same author's identical framework in the open companion paper "Development trends of the national secure PNT system based on BDS" (Yang, Ren, Jia, Sun, Science China Earth Sciences, 66(5):929-938, 2023), which I downloaded in full and text-extracted. It defines the same three-model framework the JGGS article teaches. CORE THESIS: every single PNT technology — even GNSS — is inherently vulnerable, so resilience must come from a SYSTEM-OF-SYSTEMS using DIVERSE PHYSICAL PRINCIPLES, not a single source. The framework has three nested layers: (1) COMPREHENSIVE PNT = building/integrating many heterogeneous PNT sources "seamlessly covering from deep space to deep ocean" (GNSS + LEO + Lagrange-point constellations + ground low/VLF longwave (Loran/Omega/Chayka-class) + 5G + seafloor sonar beacons + INS + pulsar/X-ray + gravity/geomagnetic/terrain matching + UWB/WiFi/BT/vision + atomic/micro clocks); (2) RESILIENT PNT = the application mode that flexibly integrates, modifies and fuses those sources via three sub-models — resilient SENSOR INTEGRATION (redundancy + autonomous selection of available sources per scene), resilient FUNCTIONAL MODEL (attach estimable "resilient correction items" to observation models to autonomously compensate scene-varying systematic errors, exploiting that sensors of different physical principles have different error sensitivities), and resilient STOCHASTIC MODEL (self-adaptive uncertainty/covariance estimation, adaptive factors in adaptive Kalman filtering, robust estimation to control outliers); (3) INTELLIGENT PNT = adding ML-driven scene perception + supervised/unsupervised learning of model errors (e.g. NLOS/multipath classification) on top. The paper is explicitly genealogical: resilient PNT descends from the author's earlier ADAPTIVE/robust estimation and adaptive-Kalman work, and the resilient stochastic model MUST rest on a reliable functional model because "any bias or outlier in the functional model will affect the stochastic model." KSHANA FIT: kshana already implements most of the COMPONENT physics this paper enumerates (multi-constellation GNSS/PVT/DOP, INS + GNSS-INS fusion, gravity/terrain/SLAM/map-matching nav, Kalman/UKF/particle filters, clock ensembles/Allan/holdover, RAIM/ARAIM integrity, lunar/Mars/deep-space PNT, link budget/PFD-relevant signal modelling). What it does NOT have, and what this source uniquely motivates, is the RESILIENCE-AS-A-PROPERTY layer: a formal resilient-functional-model mechanism (estimable correction items co-estimated across sources), a resilient-stochastic-model engine (adaptive variance/covariance + robust + adaptive-factor weighting tuned per scene), a resilient-source-selection/orchestration layer, and crucially a quantitative RESILIENCE METRIC suite (continuity/availability/reliability/integrity scored under threat, across degradation, per scene) that would turn kshana's individual simulations into a resilience-SCORING product. These are the high-leverage, on-strength, SaaS-and-standards-moat additions.

A 13-slide CGSIC 2022 vendor (Orolia) presentation by David Sohn, "A Layered Approach to Resilient PNT." Its central teaching is an explicit LAYERED DEFENSE ARCHITECTURE for resilient PNT, organized as an ordered stack of protection layers applied between the threat environment and the PNT consumer: (1) External References (GNSS + alternatives: LEO STL/XONA, network timing NTP/PTP/White Rabbit, terrestrial SoOP 5G/NextNav/Locata/TV/Radio); (2) Antennas (horizon-blocking + CRPA, providing 20-50 dB anti-jam by preventing energy reception); (3) In-Line Protections (filter/clean interference between antenna and receiver); (4) Interference Detection (detect residual interference — "detection is critical for any chance to mitigate"); (5) Internal PNT References (Rb/CSAC oscillators, MEMS/FOG IMUs as last fallback when external lost/compromised); (6) Internal Quality Detection (coherency checks using internal references to qualify external ones / find residual anomalies); plus cross-cutting Cybersecurity and Simulation & Analysis. It frames a defense-in-depth doctrine: prevent bad signals entering, clean what enters, detect residuals, fall back to shielded internal refs, qualify externals against internals. It also stresses a productisation philosophy: modular/retrofittable/upgradeable layers, scaled to risk and requirements, gradual investment in resiliency, protecting past investments. Threat generation/characterization and performance testing/certification are shown as the lifecycle bookends around the sensor/signal-fusion stack. The PNT Portal EU "Resilience Assessment" is a free, browser-based self-assessment questionnaire (~40 questions, 1-2 hours, answers stored only locally in the browser, nothing collected/shared) that directly operationalizes the US DHS S&T "Resilient PNT Conformance Framework" (v2.0, 31 May 2022) for a European audience. It scores a PNT system/installation across SEVEN resilience technique categories — Obfuscate, Limit, Verify, Isolate, Diversify, Mitigate, Recover — each producing a "Resilience Coverage Rating" (a percentage), and rolls the answers up into a single cumulative "Resilience Level" from 0 to 4. The levels are behaviorally defined and cumulative: L0 non-resilient (accepts unverified input, manual recovery only); L1 ensures recoverability after threat removal (input verification to international standards + full system recovery with memory reset + secure firmware update); L2 provides a (possibly unbounded-degradation) solution during threat (adds spoofer/malware-source identification & neglection + automatic recovery); L3 provides a solution with BOUNDED degradation during threat (adds isolation of corrupted-vs-clean source data + cross-verification between PNT sources); L4 provides a solution WITHOUT degradation during threat (adds PNT-source-technology diversification against common-mode threats). The DHS parent framework groups the techniques under three top-level behavior families — Prevent, Respond, Recover. The assessment teaches a concrete, named, questionnaire-driven methodology and a 0-4 resilience-level rubric. Crucially: the portal does NOT publish how the coverage-rating percentages are computed (no formula/weights disclosed), and provides no quantitative performance thresholds, failure-mode analysis, or test procedures — it is a self-attestation tool, not a measurement/test harness. THAT GAP is exactly kshana's opening: kshana can deterministically SIMULATE the threats and EVIDENCE the behaviors (verify, isolate, diversify, mitigate, recover) that the questionnaire only asks the operator to self-declare, turning self-attestation into measured, reproducible conformance evidence. For the founder's SaaS-suite ambition this source is high-leverage on two axes: (1) it hands kshana a published, government-backed scoring rubric to implement as a "Resilience Score / conformance report" product (standards moat + compliance/insurer/regulator report surface), and (2) it reveals an adjacent productisation surface (the assessment-as-a-service web app itself, of which there is already a live EU exemplar to differentiate against by being evidence-based rather than self-declared).

A conceptual/methodology white paper by Andy Proctor (RethinkPNT; former UKSA GNSS Technical Director, RIN PNT Advisory Group chair) that adapts Donald Firesmith's software-system-resilience framework to PNT. Its core contribution is a STRUCTURED FRAMEWORK, not algorithms: (1) a precise definition of PNT system resilience as the degree to which a system "rapidly and effectively protects its critical capabilities from harm caused by threats"; (2) decomposition of resilience into four functions — RESISTANCE (passive), DETECTION, RESPONSE, RECOVERY (active) — plus the anticipate/assess extensions; (3) a threat taxonomy (jamming, spoofing, meaconing, multipath, cyber, space-weather/natural hazards, human/system errors, defect-related failures, beyond-life) classified by threat vector, persistence and geography; (4) a requirements-engineering process (identify assets -> potential harm -> max acceptable harm -> prioritise -> develop resilience requirements -> determine/prioritise threats -> derive quality requirements) with concrete requirement TEMPLATES and a timeline of detect/respond/recover parameters (detection time, reaction start/duration, recovery start/duration, loss/degradation time vs threshold/objective); (5) a resilience-technique taxonomy classified by autonomy (autonomous/hybrid/manual), function (resist/detect/respond/recover), and composition (subsystem/HW/SW/data), with a full redundancy taxonomy and a non-exhaustive Annex-2 catalogue of ~50 named techniques; (6) four VERIFICATION methods (inspection, analysis, demonstration, testing) with black/white/grey-box resilience testing and a list of analysis CASES (robustness/safety/security/survivability/accuracy-integrity/interoperability with MTBCF, MTTR, RUL, FMECA, FTA, HAZOP, STPA/STAMP, attack-tree/attack-surface, misuse cases); and (7) the PNT ASSURANCE CASE concept ("Assured PNT" = a documented body of evidence) tied to MoD's Performance/Security/Resilience/Robustness claim categories and feeding the IEEE Resilient PNT standards effort. For kshana, the load-bearing opportunity is the assessment/scoring/assurance-reporting/requirements-traceability layer — it maps almost exactly onto kshana's identified SaaS gap (resilience scoring vs a published framework, auditable reports for regulators/insurers), whereas most individual detection/monitoring techniques are already implemented in the engine.

Discipline as strategy

What kshana should refuse to build

The most valuable output of an honest review is the list of attractive traps. These are where incumbents are entrenched, where kshana has zero heritage, or where building would force overclaiming RF/hardware fidelity it cannot prove. Staying software-pure is the moat.

✗ Hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) / RF record-and-replay testbed (as a kshana-built capability)

Building HIL requires SDR/instrument drivers, RF I/O, real-time 1000Hz guarantees, and timing/sync hardware — squarely the incumbents' turf (Safran GSG-8/Skydel, Orolia, Calnex) where their hardware moat is real and kshana has zero heritage. It pulls kshana away from its honest deterministic-software identity into a domain where it cannot claim flight-grade/certified value and would force overclaiming RF fidelity. The ONLY honest play is the one-way scenario/IQ EXPORT bridge (kept in-scope above); do not build or own the HIL layer.

✗ CRPA / wavefront / controlled-reception-pattern antenna simulation (full)

CRPA/wavefront is fundamentally an RF, multi-channel, phase-coherent shared-LO HARDWARE problem (Safran GSG-Wavefront). A software-pure engine could at most model abstract array geometry / spatial-nulling math, which delivers little real anti-jam test value without RF hardware. XL effort, narrow demand, high overclaim risk. Skip unless a specific customer drives an abstract array-geometry/spatial-nulling feature that honestly feeds the 'Antennas' layer of the architecture score.

✗ Over-the-air / anechoic radiated-test orchestration

Requires RF transmit hardware, ranges, and regulatory authorization kshana does not and should not own. The only honest overlap is the scenario-export bridge (already in-scope). Mark explicitly as skip-as-product, do-via-export.

✗ Real-time / live RFI monitoring + alerting dashboard (streaming threat monitor, RFI maps)

Kshana is explicitly a DETERMINISTIC, REPRODUCIBLE OFFLINE SIMULATOR. A live monitor needs real-time data feeds, sensor/receiver integration, streaming/persistence/alerting infra, and an ops/SRE burden that are a different product class and engineering discipline — and the space is crowded by entrenched hardware/feed incumbents (Safran BroadShield/BroadSense, GPSPATRON, Spirent, Septentrio) who own the sensors kshana lacks. The detection ALGORITHMS are reusable, but building the live-monitoring SaaS would dilute the reproducible-engine identity. Defer indefinitely; at most, later, CONSUME third-party live telemetry to continuously update a resilience score, never build the sensing layer.

✗ Managed threat/RFI/spoofing DATA feed (API subscription intelligence product)

Requires a sensor network or third-party data kshana does not own — the most data-dependent, slowest-to-bootstrap idea in the dataset, and a network-effect moat that presupposes the live-monitoring layer kshana is skipping. Do not pursue before (and likely ever, given the deterministic-engine identity); not a quick win and off-thesis.

✗ Hardware-layer resilience techniques (EM shielding, surge protectors, radiation hardening, anti-jam hardware)

Physical/hardware mitigations a software simulator cannot legitimately simulate or claim; presenting them as kshana capabilities would breach the no-TRL/no-heritage honesty rule. At most model the abstract EFFECT (e.g. antenna nulling as a configurable input parameter, honestly labelled), but do not build these as product capabilities — boundary marker keeping kshana honestly scoped as software/analysis.

✗ Re-implementing Annex-2 / common detection techniques kshana already has (RAIM, FDI, AGC/C-N0 power monitor, clock-rate monitor, Kalman ensembles, voting/failover, checksums)

Kshana's baseline ALREADY covers the substantive simulable items (RAIM/ARAIM, spoof detection/monitors covering AoA/signal-quality/power-variation/stationary checks, AGC/power monitoring, clock-rate/holdover/ensembles via Kalman/UKF/particle, voting/fusion, time-transfer integrity). Re-labelling these as 'new' would violate the honesty mandate and duplicate the engine. Only cherry-pick the 1-2 genuinely-absent niche checks (e.g. explicit position-velocity consistency monitor) as trivial adds; otherwise skip — they already underpin existing scenarios and the technique-catalogue mapping (in-scope) is the right framing.

✗ Generic standalone questionnaire/survey engine (config-driven, PNT-decoupled)

A general-purpose survey builder dilutes kshana into commodity survey software with abundant competitors and zero PNT or simulation moat. The value is the PNT-SPECIFIC, evidence-backed, simulation-driven questionnaire (in-scope); a generic engine has no differentiation. Skip unless it falls out trivially from the resilience-score feature.

✗ Generic RAMS/safety-engineering analysis primitives (MTBCF/MTTR/RUL/FMECA/FTA/HAZOP/STPA)

Standard systems/safety-engineering methods, not PNT-specific, well-served by mature dedicated tools and outside kshana's PNT-physics strength; implementing them honestly would require domain depth kshana lacks and would overstate it as a safety suite. At most ACCEPT user-supplied MTBF/MTTR figures as inputs to availability/continuity calculations — do not build the analysis engines.

Adding ML model-error learning (NLOS/multipath classification, learned weight policies) cuts against kshana's determinism and 'validated-vs-external-oracle' ethos and is the highest-overclaim-risk item — the field itself frames intelligent PNT as the OUTER layer (resilient need not be intelligent). Cyber/network-security posture is outside kshana's physics-simulation strength; deep cyber simulation would be over-claiming.
