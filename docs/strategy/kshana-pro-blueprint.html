Dominance plan · internal only

The dominance plan: own PNT assurance and make Kshana impossible to ignore

Internal wording. "Dominance" and "own the market" are Ashforde's internal goals (founder; D-K14). They stay in private documents like this one. Public copy, decks and the site compare on facts and never say "dominate" or "certified".

The goal is market dominance in PNT assurance: Kshana becomes the reference that receivers, networks and missions are measured against. Arksa owns everything Kshana is, and the parts that make the lead hard to copy never leave Ashforde.

1. Be the reference

Open formats (kshana.obs/1, mapping files) and a conformance kit others can run. Signed, pre-registered scorecards with sealed holdouts and the Jammertest referee. When a receiver maker or a network operator wants to show what their system does, Kshana's scorecard is the evidence they reach for.

2. Breadth no one else has in one engine

Receivers, interference, timing, the signal layer, LEO PNT, space RF, lunar and integrity in one verified engine: 92 kinds and 401 verification rows today. 0.41 adds monitoring networks, surveillance of space RF from orbit, and feeds from five industries (maritime, telecom, grid, aviation, finance).

3. Own the physics

Native models replace third-party tables, in this order: ionosphere, clocks and holdover, troposphere, receivers and interference. Each is pre-registered, scored against an external oracle and listed in the model register. One physics source serves Kshana and Arksa. Misses are published, which is what makes the passes credible.

4. Proof that becomes a habit

Signed, verifiable verdicts and audit packs mapped to NIST IR 8323 and the timing standards. Once operators, insurers and auditors file Kshana verdicts as evidence, switching away means re-proving everything.

5. A moat that learns

Core methods are held as trade secrets and run only as Ashforde's service. Joint fleet calibration, cross-layer cueing and threat-aware planning get better with every Station and Arksa mission (opt-in, under contract). The threat library is refreshed monthly. Competitors can copy the open engine, not the Core.

6. Distribution for free, payment for jobs

AGPL, 19 languages, free in every Arksa plan, free Desktop and one free Station. Pro and Enterprise are sold by the job they do (fleets, ledger, forecasts, scorecards), and Core is reached through Arksa. Patents are filed before anything ships in Pro.

Three layers

Open spreads it, Pro is what teams pay for, Core never ships

Open

  • AGPL, or commercial; free in every Arksa plan
  • Every reader and format, mapping files, conformance kit, schemas
  • All 92 kinds and their evidence
  • Single-site detection and alerts; free Desktop and one Station

Pro Enterprise

  • Runs on the customer's machines, unlocked by signed tokens
  • Station fleet (2 or more), area trust, history, reports
  • Threat library subscription; planner with cost
  • Signed, pre-registered referee scorecards
  • Enterprise: HA, single sign-on, audit, air-gapped bundle
  • Verifiable verdicts and time to breach join after filing

Core

  • Kshana + Arksa only: never in a download, never unlocked by a token
  • Private repository kshana-core, owned by the Kshana seat
  • Reached by Arksa's server and Ashforde's hosted service over an authenticated API
  • Never compiled to WebAssembly, the desktop sidecar or the CLI; the Pro build never links it (CI check)
  • Held as trade secret; patents where filing helps

Never paywalled: safety-relevant detection, file formats, and published evidence. Sovereign customers reach Core through an Ashforde-hosted endpoint by default; on-premises Core only under a founder-approved contract (confidentiality, no reverse engineering, audit rights, signed server image, no source).

Models

What the engine models

Receivers and trust

Receiver logs from NMEA, UBX, SBF, NovAtel, RTCM3, RINEX 2–4, Android and GNSS-SDR; trust scoring, self-reports, sky geometry and DOP; Kshana Connect maps any other log with no code.

Interference

Jamming with CRPA antennas, terrain shadowing, acquisition hysteresis and front-end quantisation; spoofing and its detection; emitter regions from ADS-B and AIS; the Jammertest referee.

Timing

Clocks, holdover and Allan statistics; telecom timing masks; measured logs from RINEX clock, TICC, TimeLab, linuxptp, chrony, ntpd and White Rabbit.

Signal layer (IQ)

Scene synthesis, acquisition, tracking loops, sweeps, front ends, lab fits from IQ, campaigns and live monitoring of growing recordings.

LEO PNT and space RF

LEO fusion, PPP and navigation messages; in 0.41, geolocation from orbit, revisit and tip-and-cue latency, AIS-validated target maintenance, dark-vessel integrity and jammers seen from orbit.

Lunar and deep space

Lunar frames and datum, LunaNet service volume, laser ranging, station observability, CR3BP families, Artemis I and CAPSTONE cross-checks against public ephemerides.

Integrity and navigation

ARAIM reproduction, hybrid integrity, inertial and terrain, gravity and combined alternative PNT, sky and skyline geometry.

Evidence

401 verification rows with status and oracle; pre-registered bars before data; signed evidence packs; identical bytes on 1 and N cores and across platforms.

Novelty

Candidate inventions for a patent attorney

Do not share outside Ashforde. Europe requires absolute novelty: anything published, including a shared link, a deck or a post, can become prior art against Ashforde's own filing. Every candidate starts in Core. P1 and P2 move to Pro only after they are filed, because a shipped binary can be studied; P3–P5 stay in Core as trade secrets. None goes into the AGPL engine.
