01

PNT, starting from nothing

PNT stands for three questions every machine that moves or coordinates needs answered: Where am I? (Positioning), How do I get there? (Navigation), and What time is it, exactly? (Timing).

In plain wordsYour phone's map showing a blue dot, your car's sat-nav, a plane staying on course, and a power grid keeping its electricity in sync — all of them are PNT. Get PNT wrong and ships run aground, drones get lost, banks mis-stamp trades, and 5G towers fall out of step.

The three letters, one at a time

📍

Positioning

Knowing where something is — latitude, longitude, altitude. Usually from GNSS (satellite signals: GPS, Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou). Your receiver listens to several satellites and triangulates.

🧭

Navigation

Knowing which way and keeping a path over time — velocity, heading, route. When satellites disappear, you "dead-reckon" with onboard inertial sensors that feel motion.

⏱️

Timing

Knowing what time it is to the nanosecond (a billionth of a second). GPS itself is really a clock in the sky; timing also synchronises grids, finance and telecoms.

Why timing is secretly the hardest part

Position from satellites is really a timing trick: a receiver measures how long a signal took to arrive, multiplies by the speed of light, and gets distance. Light travels ~30 cm per nanosecond — so a clock error of a few nanoseconds becomes a position error of metres. That is why everything hinges on atomic clocks and on measuring how steady a clock is.

AnalogyA clock's quality isn't "is it right now?" — it's "if I unplug its reference, how fast does it drift?" Think of a wristwatch left in a drawer: a cheap one is minutes off by next week; an atomic clock would be off by less than a second in millions of years. The standard ruler for that steadiness is the Allan deviation — a curve showing the clock's error at 1 second, 10 seconds, an hour, a day. Kshana computes exactly this curve, and we've checked it against real measured clocks.

Why PNT gets hard — and where the money/risk is

🛰️ Beyond Earth (Moon, deep space)

GPS only works near Earth. On the Moon there is no GPS, so spacecraft need their own clocks, their own way to figure out their orbit (orbit determination), and a new lunar time scale. ESA's "Moonlight" programme is building exactly this.

🚫 GNSS-denied (jamming & attacks)

Satellite signals are faint and easily attacked: jamming (drowning them in noise), spoofing (broadcasting fake signals to send you to the wrong place), meaconing (rebroadcasting real signals with a delay). Defence, aviation and shipping need PNT that survives this.

Where "quantum" enters

New quantum devices promise better PNT: optical/quantum clocks far steadier than today's caesium clocks, and cold-atom interferometers — sensors that measure acceleration and gravity by the wave-like behaviour of ultra-cold atoms, so a vehicle can navigate accurately for much longer without GPS. They are early-stage and expensive. The big question agencies ask is: do they actually help, by how much, and when are they ready? That question is what the main ESA tender (and Kshana) is built to answer.

You now know the field. PNT = position/navigation/timing; it runs on satellites and atomic clocks; it breaks in space and under attack; and quantum devices might fix that. Everything below is about a tool that lets you test all of this in software, honestly.
02

What Kshana is and what it delivers

Kshana is a software simulation engine for PNT. You describe a scenario — a clock, a spacecraft, an attack, a quantum sensor — and Kshana computes how well it would perform, and crucially, how much to trust that answer.

AnalogyIt is a flight simulator for PNT systems. A pilot trains in a simulator before flying a real jet; we test a quantum clock or a cold-atom navigator in Kshana before anyone spends years and millions building hardware. The difference from a toy simulator: Kshana labels every number with how it was checked.

What it delivers (the outputs)

▶️

Runnable scenarios

40+ ready scenario "kinds" — run one and get a result as data (JSON), a chart (SVG) and a human summary. Anyone can reproduce it from the same inputs.

⚖️

Trade studies

Quantum-vs-classical head-to-heads: "the quantum clock holds time to 1.2 ps where the classical one drifts to 5.3 ns" — each comparison carries an honesty label.

📦

Evidence packs

Bundled, reproducible studies + reports that become the backbone of a research paper or a tender bid.

How it works, end to end

You pick a scenario. e.g. quantum-time-transfer, plus inputs (link loss, integration time) and a random seed.
The engine routes it. A single typed "dispatcher" sends it to the right physics modules (clocks, orbits, detectors…).
The modules compute. Reusing checked building blocks (the clock-stability maths, the detector statistics, the orbit solver).
It returns a result + an honesty record. Numbers, a chart, and a "representativeness" note saying what's real, what's assumed, and what's missing before flight.
In plain wordsSame inputs + same seed + same version → exactly the same answer, every time, on anyone's machine. That reproducibility is what makes the results trustworthy rather than just plausible.
03

The honesty engine — why this is the whole game

Most simulators can claim anything. Kshana cannot: it labels every capability, and an automated gate fails the build if a claim isn't backed. This discipline is not paperwork — it is the product's main advantage.

Three honesty labels

Validated — checked against real, independent data or a trusted reference tool Modelled — built from textbook physics, but not yet checked against real data Partner-owned — we don't build it; honestly declared as a gap
In plain wordsValidated = "we compared our answer to reality and it matched." Modelled = "this is correct textbook physics, but we haven't held it next to a real measurement yet." We never call something Validated to look good. Being openly Modelled where we are Modelled is what makes the Validated claims believable.

"Validated islands" — results checked against the real world

The "representativeness + gaps-to-flight" ledger

Every quantum demonstration carries a machine-checked note with four parts: what's anchored to reality, what we assumed, what's still missing before real flight, and a realistic readiness band. Nothing claims to be flight-ready when it isn't.

Why honesty is the moat. A buyer or a space agency can audit every Kshana claim themselves — the code is open. Competitors who overclaim cannot survive that audit. So our restraint ("Modelled unless proven; no flight heritage; no certification") is precisely what makes us the trustworthy option. Open by design, fast by intent, proven before trusted.

Where information flows (and what stays private)

Two simple rules govern everything: capability flows OUT to the open repo; evidence, strategy and money flow IN to the private one. Public results are cited by private bids — the private side never copies the engine code.

What leaves the building (public)

The engine, the methods, the validated results. Anyone — including a competitor or an ESA reviewer — can run and verify them. That is the point.

What never leaves (private)

Tender-specific framing and numbers, the winning strategy, the curated evidence packs, pricing and contracts. None of this is needed to use the engine — only to win with it.

07

ESA tenders, explained from zero

A tender is how a big customer buys work. The European Space Agency (ESA) writes down a need, invites companies to propose how they'd do it, and picks. Winning one funds the work and builds a flight-grade track record.

The vocabulary (you'll see these everywhere)

ITT / AO

Invitation To Tender / Announcement of Opportunity — the published call. Ours is AO/1-13503.

SoW

Statement of Work — what ESA wants done, broken into requirements (e.g. "§1.5.2: three application areas").

Clarifications

Official Q&A during the bid window. ESA's answers can change the rules — and ours did, in our favour.

Consortium & WP

Tenders are usually won by a team (consortium); each partner owns a Work Package (WP). We enter as the software/simulation WP.

TRL 1–9

Technology Readiness Level: 1 = idea, 9 = flown in space. We are honestly around TRL 3 (proof-of-concept). Overstating TRL is fatal.

ESA-STAR

ESA's bidder portal. Our entity (Ashforde OÜ) is registered there, which is required to bid.

Our flagship: AO/1-13503 "Quantum-Enabled PNT"

ESA wants an independent, credible assessment of whether quantum tech improves PNT across timing, navigation and anomaly detection — feasibility, performance, and operational benefit. Bid deadline: 17 Aug 2026.

Glossary — every term, in one place

PNT
Positioning, Navigation, Timing — where you are, which way you're going, and the exact time.
GNSS
Global Navigation Satellite System — the satellites behind sat-nav: GPS (US), Galileo (EU), GLONASS (RU), BeiDou (CN).
Atomic clock
A clock that keeps time using atoms; extraordinarily stable. Caesium is today's workhorse; optical/quantum clocks are the next step.
Allan deviation (ADEV)
The standard ruler for clock stability — how much a clock drifts over 1s, 10s, an hour, a day. Lower is better.
Holdover
How long a clock stays accurate after it loses its reference signal. Quantum clocks promise much longer holdover.
Inertial / IMU / INS
Sensors that feel motion (acceleration, rotation) so you can navigate without GPS by "dead-reckoning" — but error grows over time.
Cold-atom interferometer
A quantum sensor measuring acceleration/gravity via the wave behaviour of ultra-cold atoms; can extend GNSS-free navigation.
DOP
Dilution of Precision — how good the satellite geometry is. Spread-out satellites = sharp fix; clustered = fuzzy fix.
RAIM / integrity
The receiver checking itself for bad measurements, and quantifying how much to trust the answer — vital for aviation.
Jamming
Drowning satellite signals in noise so receivers lose lock.
Spoofing
Broadcasting fake satellite signals to trick a receiver into a wrong position or time.
Meaconing
Capturing real signals and rebroadcasting them with a delay to mislead a receiver.
Orbit determination (OD)
Working out a spacecraft's orbit from measurements — essential where there's no GPS (e.g. the Moon).
Ephemeris
The published table of where a satellite is over time.
ROC / AUC
A score (0.5 = coin-flip, 1.0 = perfect) for how well a detector separates "fault" from "normal".
Validated / Modelled
Kshana's honesty labels: checked against real data vs. correct-but-unchecked-against-reality.
Representativeness ledger
The note on each demo stating what's real, what's assumed, and what's missing before flight.
Open-core
A business model: the core engine is open-source; the strategy, evidence and commercial layer are private.
AGPL-3.0
A strong open-source licence; we also sell a separate commercial licence for those who can't use AGPL.
ESA
European Space Agency — Europe's space agency and our target customer.
Tender / ITT / AO
The formal "here's our need, bid for it" process and its published call.
SoW
Statement of Work — the list of what the tender requires.
Consortium / Work Package
A team of companies bidding together; each owns a slice of the work (a WP).
TRL
Technology Readiness Level 1–9 (idea → flown). We are honestly ~3.
Product Assurance (PA)
The quality/safety process a space project must follow; ours is automated in the build.
SBOM
Software Bill of Materials — a complete list of what's inside the software, for supply-chain trust.
