This is a paper study, not a build contract: it produces requirements, trade-offs, feasibility and a preliminary design that justify a much larger follow-on demonstrator. It runs as the sister tender to AO/1-13503 (Quantum-Enabled PNT) — same programme, deadline, budget shape and rules, but bid separately (combined proposals are forbidden).

1What ESA is actually asking for

Today's PNT (GNSS) payloads are largely fixed-function once launched. ESA wants to study how artificial intelligence and digital / reconfigurable payload technology can make the next generation of PNT spacecraft smarter, adaptable in orbit, and resilient to interference — and how to predict and verify that performance on the ground before committing to flight.

Concretely, the bidder must investigate and mature the building blocks of an AI-and-digital-payload PNT demonstrator, then converge on a preliminary design for an in-orbit demonstration. The single most load-bearing concept is the Navigation Digital Twin (NAV-DT): a ground-segment model that predicts the demonstrator's achievable PNT performance and resilience, and that verifies the on-board algorithms before and during the mission.

Why this matters for us: the NAV-DT — a model that predicts PNT performance/resilience and verifies algorithms — is almost a one-line description of what Kshana already is. The fit, and the parts that are squarely a partner's, are laid out in 13494 · our fit.

2The technical scope — the themes it covers

The study spans the on-board side, the ground side, and the methods that tie them together:

On-board AI for PNT
AI/ML running on the payload for anomaly & RF-impairment detection-classification (jamming, spoofing, multipath), autonomy, and in-orbit adaptation — under real edge-compute constraints.
Digital / reconfigurable payload
Software-defined, in-orbit-reconfigurable PNT payload technology (SDR/FPGA, secure in-orbit update of signals and algorithms).
Navigation Digital Twin (NAV-DT)
A ground model that predicts achievable PNT performance/resilience and verifies algorithms, integrating with the wider data infrastructure ("SpaceStream"/NAVEX-style aggregated metrics).
ML technique characterisation & evaluation
A rigorous way to characterise and evaluate machine-learning techniques for PNT — how good is a detector, really, and how do you prove it without fooling yourself.
RF-environment & impairment modelling
Representative jamming / spoofing / multipath / ionosphere scenarios (incl. DestinE-style environment twins) to test against.
System, payload & ground architecture
Spacecraft bus, antenna/RF payload hardware, core ground infrastructure, PNT data lakehouse — and the system engineering, PA/QA and verification that bind it.

The scope deliberately mixes flight hardware (bus, antennas, payload, SDR/FPGA), ground software (NAV-DT, AI evaluation, data infrastructure) and method/assurance work — which is why it is built for a multi-disciplinary consortium, not a single supplier.

3How the study runs — phases & key deliverables

Phase 0
≈mo 0–4
Mission analysis & scenario. System scenario, benefits analysis vs state-of-the-art, mission-concept definition. Closes at the Mission Definition Review (MDR). Deliverables incl. D1.1 (scenario + benefits + tech SoTA), D1.3 (mission-concept trade-off), D1.4 (Mission Requirement Document).
Phase A
≈mo 4–11
Feasibility. Feasibility assessment of the AI / digital-payload / NAV-DT concept and architecture. Closes at the Preliminary Requirements Review (PRR). Deliverables incl. D2.6 (feasibility), D2.16 (NAV-DT architecture + digital-twin integration feasibility), plus the SE/PA spine (D2.2 PA/QA plan, D2.3 SE plan).
🚦 GATEKEEPER (≈ KO+12) — a hard go / no-go between the two funded phases. Only what survives this gate is taken into the preliminary design.
Phase B1
≈mo 13–30
Preliminary definition. Build the demonstrator's preliminary design and the supporting tools (NAV-DT component, AI-evaluation testbed), run the algorithms through them, and define the IOD. Closes at the Interim System Requirements Review (ISRR) and a Final Review.

The full ECSS document tree is replaced by a tailored Document Requirements List (DRL); top-level products include a System Requirement Document, System Design Report, a Design/Development & Verification Plan, and an Operations Manual.

4Who can bid — the rules that decide eligibility

Participating States (geographical eligibility)

Bidders and every economic operator must come from the FutureNAV participating states: BE · CA · CH · CZ · DE · ES · GB · IT · LV · NL · NO · PL · SE. Funding is returned to states roughly in proportion to their contribution, with Germany the largest share.

Mandatory consortium shape (not a single supplier)

  • ≥ 5 economic operators including the prime;
  • ≥ 3 SMEs in the team;
  • ≥ 30% of the budget to non-large-system-integrator operators;
  • ≥ €500K placed through genuine open competition;
  • ≥ 1 mandatory Key Person with extensive space-segment ("spacecraft-as-a-service") experience — every work-package manager is a named Key Person with a CV.
