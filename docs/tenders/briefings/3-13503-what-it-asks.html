Like its sister tender, this is a study that produces requirements, a quantum-vs-classical trade, feasibility and a preliminary design — the homework that justifies a far larger flight demonstrator. Runs alongside AO/1-13494 (AI & Digital Payload) under identical commercial terms; bid separately (combined proposals are forbidden).

1What ESA is actually asking for

Europe's PNT depends on GNSS, which can be jammed, spoofed or simply unavailable. Quantum sensors promise navigation that holds up when GNSS does not — atomic clocks stable enough to keep time through long outages, and cold-atom inertial sensors that drift far less than conventional gyros and accelerometers.

ESA wants a bidder to answer, rigorously: what real PNT benefit does a quantum payload buy over the best classical alternative, in which mission scenarios, and is an in-orbit demonstrator feasible? The study must define the use cases and requirements, perform a defensible quantum-vs-classical performance trade, assess feasibility of the quantum payload and the mission, and produce a preliminary design heading toward an IOD.

Why this matters for us: the heart of the study is a defensible, reproducible quantum-vs-classical PNT performance trade and a GNSS-denied resilience case — exactly the ground-side, performance-simulation half of the problem. See 13503 · our fit for what is ours and what is squarely the hardware partner's.

2The technical scope — the themes it covers

Quantum timing
Space-grade optical / atomic clocks and their stability (Allan deviation, holdover), and quantum / optical time transfer between platforms.
Quantum inertial sensing
Cold-atom interferometer (CAI) accelerometers & gyros for low-drift dead-reckoning when GNSS is denied.
Quantum-vs-classical PNT trade
The core question: how much PNT performance does quantum buy over a classical baseline (nav-grade INS + chip-scale/USO clock), and where.
GNSS-denied / resilient PNT
Position/timing error growth under jamming, spoofing and full outage; alternative-PNT (e.g. map-matching) bounding the drift.
Mission & constellation analysis
Orbit/constellation design, coverage, availability and integrity for the candidate IOD, plus the operations concept.
Quantum payload & spacecraft engineering
Quantum-sensor hardware maturation (TRL), the spacecraft bus, product assurance and the path to flight.

As with 13494, the scope spans quantum hardware, system/bus engineering, and the ground-side performance/trade analysis — built for a multi-disciplinary consortium with genuine quantum-hardware depth at its core.

3How the study runs — phases & key deliverables

Phase 0
≈mo 0–4
Mission analysis. Use cases, system scenario, benefits analysis, mission-concept definition; closes at the Mission Definition Review (MDR). Contributes the benefits / resilience case (D1.1-class) and mission sizing.
Phase A
≈mo 4–11
Feasibility. Quantum-vs-classical trade, payload & mission feasibility, preliminary requirements; closes at the Preliminary Requirements Review (PRR).
🚦 GATEKEEPER go / no-go between the funded phases — only what survives is carried into preliminary definition.
Phase B1
≈mo 13–30
Preliminary definition. Preliminary design of the quantum-enabled demonstrator and its verification approach; closes at the Interim System Requirements Review (ISRR) and a Final Review.

Top-level products (the ECSS document tree is replaced by a tailored project DRL): a System Requirement Document, a System Design Report, a Design/Development & Verification Plan (DDVP), and an Operations Manual, plus the SE / PA / QA spine.

4Who can bid — the rules that decide eligibility

Participating States (geographical eligibility)

Bidders and every economic operator must come from the FutureNAV participating states: BE · CA · CZ · DE · IT · LV · NO · PL · ES · SE · CH · NL · UK. Geo-return is led by Germany (≈48.9%), then Switzerland (≈22%), Italy, Sweden and others — a strong steer toward German-anchored, quantum-capable teams.

Mandatory consortium shape

  • ≥ 5 economic operators incl. the prime, ≥ 3 SMEs, ≥ 30% of budget to non-large-system-integrators, ≥ €500K via open competition;
  • ≥ 1 mandatory Key Person with extensive experience developing quantum technologies for navigation — every WP manager is a named Key Person with a CV;
  • full system-engineering breadth (AOCS, thermal, structures, propulsion, RF payload, EEE/power) and space-hardware heritage; Class-Gamma product assurance.
