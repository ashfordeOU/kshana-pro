## 1. What D1.1 answers

The demonstrator's central operational question is: **when GNSS is denied (jammed,
spoofed, or simply unavailable), how long can a quantum-enabled candidate coast
before its position and timing errors exceed budget, and how much better is that than
a classical baseline?** D1.1 answers it on two axes, both runnable today in the open
engine:

1. **Position drift over a GNSS-denied coast** — the unaided inertial dead-reckoning
   error vs time, for a quantum CAI accelerometer vs a classical navigation-grade INS.
2. **Timing holdover** — the coast time to a timing-error threshold, for an optical
   clock vs a classical chip-scale atomic clock (CSAC) reference.

Both axes are then **composed** into a single GNSS-denied resilience-vs-time envelope,
with the (finite) coast time to a position-equivalent threshold as the headline figure
of merit. The benefit is reported as a candidate ÷ baseline ratio.

This is the system-performance half of the trade. The device-feasibility half — can a
flight-representative CAI and optical clock actually achieve the input parameters — is
the quantum-hardware partner's WP, and D1.1 *ingests* their parameters rather than
asserting them.

## 3. Modelled axis 1 — position drift over a GNSS-denied coast

### 3.1 The model and where it lives in the open core

The quantum-inertial dead-reckoning budget is `QuantumNavBudget`
(`src/inertial/quantum_imu.rs`). It composes three independent error sources over a
coast time `t` from a perfect GNSS-aligned start, as the root-sum-square of their 1-σ
contributions:

- **Velocity random walk (VRW)** from the CAI white-acceleration PSD `q_va`:
  position-error variance `q_va·t³/3` (double-integrated white acceleration noise) →
  position drift grows as `t^1.5`. The `q_va` is **derived from the interferometer
  physics from first principles** (`CaiAccelerometer`): the effective Raman wavevector
  `k_eff = 4π/λ`, the Mach–Zehnder phase `Φ = k_eff·a·T²`, the quantum projection
  (shot) noise `σ_Φ = 1/(C·√N)`, the per-shot acceleration sensitivity
  `σ_a = σ_Φ/(k_eff·T²)`, and the shot-noise-limited acceleration ASD
  `n_a = σ_a·√T_c`, whose square is `q_va`.
- **Residual accelerometer bias** `b` (post-calibration): position error `½·b·t²`.
- **Scale-factor error** `ε` acting on the sustained specific force: `½·ε·a_ref·t²`
  (vanishes in free fall, where `a_ref ≈ 0` — bias dominates in microgravity).

The **inertial holdover** — the coast time at which the total 1-σ drift first reaches
a position threshold — is the inversion of that (monotone) drift curve
(`QuantumNavBudget::holdover_seconds`).

### 3.2 Honest grounding of the CAI floor

The shot-noise floor the budget consumes is a **fundamental lower bound**, not a
device achievement. The open engine carries an explicit bracket check against the
Freier et al. 2016 mobile quantum gravimeter (arXiv:1512.05660, 96 nm/s²/√Hz
short-term): the modelled shot-noise floor lies **below** the achieved noise and
**within ~2 orders of magnitude** of it (the real device is vibration/technical-noise
limited, not at the standard quantum limit). The engine also models the **vibration
floor** (`σ_a,vib = √(S_a/(3T))`), which on any real platform dominates shot noise —
so D1.1 is explicit that a flight CAI's effective `q_va` is set by platform vibration
and the quantum-hardware partner's device, **not** by the optimistic shot-noise floor.

### 3.3 Illustrative result (modelled)

The shape of the benefit, reproduced in the engine's regression tests
(`quantum_trade::tests::quantum_ins_coasts_longer_than_classical_and_trade_reports_benefit`):
a quantum CAI budget (tiny residual bias, sub-µg/√Hz floor) coasts **materially
longer** than a classical navigation-grade INS budget (larger bias, larger VRW) before
reaching the same position threshold. In the headline FutureNAV framing already carried
in the open capability ledger, a CAI-disciplined dead-reckoning solution bounds a
120-second GNSS outage to **a few hundred metres**, where a navigation-grade *free* INS
drifts to **kilometres** over the same window. *(Modelled simulation; the exact metres
depend on the partner's device parameters fed into `q_va` and the residual bias.)*

## 4. Modelled axis 2 — timing holdover

### 4.1 The model and where it lives in the open core

The GNSS-denied clock holdover is `holdover_seconds` (`src/holdover.rs`). For the
standard power-law clock model the **phase-error variance after coasting `t` seconds**
from a perfectly known state is the van Loan process-noise growth

```
  σ_x²(t) = q_wf·t  +  q_rw·t³/3  +  q_drift·t⁵/20      (s²)
```

with `q_wf` the white-FM PSD, `q_rw` the random-walk-FM PSD and `q_drift` the
random-run/drift PSD — the same PSDs `clock_state::q_from_allan` produces from Allan
deviations. The **holdover duration** is the (monotone) inversion of that variance to a
phase-error threshold; the white-FM-only case has the exact closed form
`t = threshold²/q_wf`. A timing error maps to a one-way range error via
`phase_to_range_m` (`c·Δt`; 1 ns ≈ 0.2998 m).

The engine carries representative quantum-clock classes (`QuantumClockClass`:
optical-lattice σ_y(1 s) ≈ 5e-16, trapped-ion ≈ 1e-15, space-qualified mercury-ion
≈ 1e-13) alongside the classical `ClockClass` (CSAC/USO/DSAC), so an optical clock can
be weighed against a CSAC baseline on the same axis.

### 4.2 THE CRITICAL HOLDOVER CAVEAT (must travel with every class-based figure)

**For a very stable clock the holdover to a tight timing threshold is governed not by
the cited σ_y(1 s) but by the *assumed* long-tau red-noise floor (`q_rw`, `q_drift`).**
The `QuantumClockClass`/`ClockClass` convenience methods *synthesise* that floor two
and four decades below the white-FM ADEV — a representative **modelling assumption,
not a measured value** — and the holdover is sensitive to it (sweeping the assumed
floor a single decade can move a class holdover several-fold). The open engine makes
this explicit in a dedicated honesty test
(`holdover::tests::class_holdover_to_tight_threshold_is_floor_dominated`): white-FM
alone gives an absurd, mission-irrelevant holdover, proving the cited ADEV does **not**
set the class number; the assumed floor does.

**Consequence for D1.1:** a class-default optical-clock holdover figure is an
**order-of-magnitude bracket whose long-tau answer is floor-governed**, and is reported
as such — never as a per-unit specification. For a defensible number, the partner's
**measured** ADEV must drive the floor (§5).

### 4.3 Illustrative result (modelled, floor-bracketed)

Under the shared synthesis recipe an optical-lattice clock holds over **longer** than a
CSAC to the same timing threshold (`quantum_trade::tests::better_clock_holds_over_longer`,
`holdover::tests::holdover_ordering_follows_the_shared_floor_recipe`) — but, per §4.2,
that ordering follows the shared assumed floor and is a **consistency** statement, not
an independent physical discovery. Any headline holdover hours for a stable optical
clock are floor artefacts until measured-ADEV-grounded.

## 5. The defensibility hinge — measured-ADEV ingestion (NNLS)

The way D1.1 moves a holdover figure from "floor-assumed bracket" to **defensible** is
to drive the noise model from a partner clock's **measured overlapping-ADEV curve**
instead of a synthesised floor. The open engine does this with
`qparams_from_adev_curve` (`src/quantum_trade.rs`):

- It fits the measured `σ_y²(τ)` curve to the holdover noise model over the canonical
  Allan-variance basis `{1/τ, τ, τ³}` (white-FM, random-walk-FM, random-run-FM/drift)
  by **non-negative least squares (NNLS)** — an exact 3-variable active-set search over
  all sign-feasible basis subsets, so a PSD is never recovered negative.
- The recovered `(q_wf, q_rw, q_drift)` feed `holdover_seconds` directly.
- The fit is round-trip-checked against the engine's independent `q_from_allan`
  converter (`quantum_trade::tests::measured_adev_round_trips_against_q_from_allan_oracle`),
  which would fail on a wrong basis power (e.g. a τ² drift law).

When the floor comes from a measured ADEV, the trade artifact is tagged
`Provenance::MeasuredAdev` and the floor-assumption caveat **drops off**
(`measured_clock_trade_drops_the_floor_caveat`). When it comes from a class default, it
is tagged `Provenance::AssumedFloor` and the caveat is **carried on the artifact face**
(`floor_caveat`). This is the mechanism that keeps the holdover claim honest: the
caveat is structural, not editorial.

> **Partner action for a defensible D1.1:** supply a measured ADEV trace (τ, σ_y(τ))
> for the candidate clock. Ashforde ingests it through `qparams_from_adev_curve`; the
> resulting holdover number is then floor-governed by **data, not assumption**, and the
> caveat is removed automatically.

## 6. The composed GNSS-denied resilience envelope

The two axes are composed into one error-vs-time envelope by `resilience_envelope`
(`src/quantum_trade.rs`):

- The inertial position drift is **capped** at an alt-PNT bound `alt_pnt_bound_m` — a
  terrain / gravity / magnetic map-matching fix bounds the unaided drift. (Kshana's
  cold-atom gravity-map matcher bounds a 60-minute GNSS-denied outage to **≈ 145 m**
  where a free INS drifts to **≈ 70 km**, `src/gravimeter.rs` /
  `src/altpnt/terrain.rs`; that bound is the alt-PNT cap the envelope can be driven
  with — *modelled, synthetic / low-degree maps*.)
- The clock phase error is mapped to a range error (`c·σ_x`).
- The envelope is the RSS of the (alt-PNT-bounded) position drift and the clock range
  error, treated as independent — with the alt-PNT fix bounding **position but not
  time** (a stated modelling convention, not an assumption left implicit).

The headline FoM is the **coast time to the threshold**. Because the clock range error
grows without bound in `t` (∝√t for white-FM, faster with red noise), the envelope
**does** eventually cross for any real (noisy) clock — so the coast time is always
**finite**, never reported as literal infinity, and is searched over a finite modelled
horizon (`RESILIENCE_HORIZON_S` ≈ 116 days). A near-noiseless clock under a tight
alt-PNT bound that stays under the threshold to the horizon is reported as a **lower
bound** (`exceeds_horizon = true`), not as "bounded indefinitely". *(This honesty —
finite-coast, horizon-as-lower-bound — was a real overclaim caught and fixed in
adversarial review; the regression
`resilience_envelope_is_monotone_finite_and_horizon_honest` pins it.)*

## 7. Output of D1.1 (the evidence pack)

For a given candidate-vs-baseline definition and threshold set, D1.1 produces:

1. A **quantum-vs-classical trade table** (`quantum_vs_classical_trade` →
   `TradeResult`): timing holdover, inertial holdover, and candidate ÷ baseline
   **benefit ratios**, each row tagged with whether its clock floor is measured or
   assumed, and a `floor_caveat` rendered on the artifact whenever any row is
   floor-assumed.
2. A **resilience-vs-time envelope** (`resilience_envelope` → `ResilienceEnvelope`):
   the error-vs-time points, the finite coast-time FoM, and whether the alt-PNT bound
   is actively capping at the crossing.
3. A **MODELLED provenance banner** on every artifact face
   (`TradeResult::provenance_banner`): *"MODELLED — quantifies a partner benefit; not
   validated, no flight heritage"*.

All outputs are deterministic and scenario-hashed (reproducible from a committed
scenario + seed + pinned engine version), so an ESA reviewer can re-run them.

## 8. Honest boundaries of D1.1

Stated plainly, because honesty is the differentiator:

1. **Quantifies, never validates the device.** D1.1 computes the resilience a quantum
   sensor *would* buy from its performance parameters; it does not, and cannot,
   validate that a flight-representative CAI or optical clock achieves them. That is the
   quantum-hardware partner's WP and Key Person.
2. **Every number is MODELLED.** None of D1.1's figures borrow the engine's
   external-oracle validation islands (SGP4 / Allan-vs-Stable32 / IGS). The CAI
   physics is first-principles (bracketed, not parity-matched, against Freier 2016);
   the holdover and trade are modelled and self-/cross-checked.
3. **The holdover floor caveat is structural.** Class-based holdover figures are
   floor-governed for stable clocks and carry the caveat automatically; a defensible
   number requires the partner's measured ADEV (§5).
4. **The device and its Key Person are partner-owned.** Ashforde is a consortium-SME WP
   contributor — never the prime, never a mandated Key Person. Hardware design,
   maturation, and flight product assurance are partners' disciplines.
5. **No TRL > 3 / heritage / certification / ESA endorsement.** Kshana is a simulation
   substrate at TRL ≈ 3.
