## 3. Facts counsel will need (checked 2026-09-25)

- **Authorship of the open engine.** The GitHub contributors listing for the public
  repository `ashfordeOU/kshana` shows two contributors: `ashfordeOU` (831 commits) and
  `dependabot[bot]` (1 commit, an automated dependency-version change). Source: GitHub's public
  API, endpoint `repos/ashfordeOU/kshana/contributors`, read 2026-09-25.
- **Inbound terms.** `CONTRIBUTING.md` says that by contributing, a contributor licenses
  the contribution under AGPL-3.0-only and grants Ashforde OÜ the right to relicense it
  in the commercially licensed edition; it asks for `git commit -s` sign-off. There is
  no separately signed contributor agreement.
- **Pro embeds the engine.** `Cargo.toml` in this repository depends on the published
  `kshana` crate; its comment says Pro "embeds the whole engine and every open feature".
  Pro is distributed (or run for customers) under proprietary terms. That is lawful only
  because Ashforde OÜ holds the rights needed to license the engine other than under
  the AGPL.
- **Third-party code.** Public `LICENSING.md` says the engine's dependencies are kept
  permissive and are checked by `deny.toml`. Pro adds its own dependencies (for example
  the PDF libraries `printpdf` and `lopdf`); this repository has no equivalent licence
  check and no third-party notices file.
- **Licence enforcement in code.** Pro's `license` module checks a token signed with an
  HMAC over SHA-256. The module's own documentation says a symmetric key is acceptable
  for a self-hosted check but a distributed product should move to asymmetric signing,
  because otherwise the verifying binary holds the signing secret.
- **Contracting entity.** The company is registered in Estonia; its ESA-determined
  operative nationality is Germany (see `legal/COUNSEL-REVIEW-PACKAGE.md` section 2).

## 4. Issues found in the drafts

Each issue names the place, the problem, and why it matters. Numbers are referenced by
the questions in section 5.

1. **No grant for the engine under commercial terms.** `legal/COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`
   defines "Engine" (section 1) but section 2 grants rights only in "Pro and the Study
   Reports". Its header says open Kshana "is **not** covered here — it is licensed
   separately under AGPL-3.0 (or this commercial licence for closed/proprietary use…)",
   which says both that it is and is not covered. Public `LICENSING.md` promises
   customers a commercial licence to embed the engine in closed products, to run a
   modified engine as a network service without the AGPL section 13 duty, and to obtain
   warranty and indemnity. Nothing sells that today, and it is the product
   `LICENSING.md` advertises.
2. **Internal-use-only grant versus study dossiers meant to be shown to others.**
   Section 2 limits use to "the licensee's internal business purposes". A timing
   holdover dossier is bought precisely to show to an auditor, regulator, test house or
   the licensee's own customer. As drafted, doing so may breach the licence.
3. **Reverse-engineering ban versus mandatory law.** Section 3 bans reverse-engineering
   the proprietary overlay without exception. In the European Union, Directive
   2009/24/EC on the legal protection of computer programs makes certain acts (such as
   observing, studying and testing a program, and decompiling for interoperability)
   non-excludable by contract. The clause may need the usual carve-out.
4. **Hosting restriction and the engine.** Section 3 bans hosting "Pro as a service to
   third parties". For the engine (issue 1), hosting a modified engine as a service is
   one of the things the commercial licence is meant to **permit**. The two products
   need different restriction lists.
5. **Warranty wording tied to the AGPL.** `legal/SUPPORT-SLA.md` section 4 says the
   disclaimer is "consistent with `LICENSE` and the AGPL-3.0 terms of the embedded open
   engine". A commercial licensee does not receive the engine under the AGPL, and
   `LICENSING.md` offers commercial customers warranty terms the AGPL disclaims. The
   warranty source for the engine inside Pro should be the commercial agreement.
6. **Contribution terms may be too light for relicensing.** Relicensing contributions
   rests on a "by contributing, you agree" paragraph plus sign-off, not a signed
   agreement. Today the only non-founder commit is automated (section 3), so exposure
   is small, but it grows with the first real external pull request.
7. **Third-party notices.** Pro has no dependency-licence check and no notices file.
   Permissive licences such as the MIT (Massachusetts Institute of Technology) licence and Apache-2.0 still require their notices to travel
   with distributed binaries.
8. **Enforcement relies on contract.** Because the token is symmetric (section 3), a
   licensee holding a Pro binary could in principle mint its own token. The licence has
   no audit or usage-verification clause and no anti-circumvention clause.
9. **Fees and payment terms are blank.** Section 6 reads `[payment terms — e.g. net
   30]`. Prices are deferred by founder decision; counsel should still fix the
   mechanics: invoicing, late-payment interest (and whether the statutory rate under
   Directive 2011/7/EU on late payment applies by default), suspension for
   non-payment, and taxes.
10. **Liability cap is a placeholder and differs by document.** Section 8 says `[e.g.
    fees paid in the preceding 12 months]`; `legal/SUPPORT-SLA.md` section 6 states
    the same cap as a proposal with carve-outs "to confirm with counsel".
11. **Governing law and forum are open.** Section 10 reads `[Estonia or Germany — align
    with the MSA]`, and no MSA exists.
12. **Document hierarchy is undefined.** The order form now says the licence governs
    unless the order form "expressly says otherwise". The licence, SOW, SLA, DPA, NDA
    and a future MSA have no stated order of precedence.
13. **Export-control flow-down is thin.** Section 10 points to
    `legal/EXPORT-CONTROL-ASSESSMENT.md` and "the appropriate clearance". There is no
    licensee undertaking on re-export, end use, sanctions screening, or the right to
    suspend delivery if a determination changes. The timing holdover study sits behind
    the Pack B hold in `legal/EXPORT-CONTROL-DETERMINATION.md`.
14. **Continuity.** One maintainer writes the product (section 3). Infrastructure and
    telecom buyers commonly ask for source-code escrow or a continuity commitment.
    The draft is silent.
15. **Name and form.** The file is titled "Commercial Licence Agreement (EULA)". "End
    user licence" suggests consumer click-through terms; these are B2B terms signed by
    order form. Section 10 has no language clause and no signature mechanism.

## 5. Questions counsel must answer

Answer each with a decision and, where text changes, the replacement wording.

**Structure**

- **Q1** (issues 1, 4, 12, 15). Should the engine's commercial licence be a separate
  agreement, or a schedule of one agreement with a Pro schedule and a study-report
  schedule? Draft the engine grant: embedding in closed products, running a modified
  engine as a network service, and redistribution inside the licensee's products.
- **Q2** (issues 11, 12). Is order form + licence + SOW (+ SLA, DPA, NDA) a sound
  contract set without an MSA? If not, draft the MSA. Either way, state the order of
  precedence.
- **Q3** (issue 11). Which governing law and forum: Estonian (place of registration),
  German (operative nationality), or the customer's? Courts or arbitration, where, in
  which language?

**Rights and restrictions**

- **Q4** (issue 2). Draft a permitted-disclosure clause so study dossiers can be shown
  to the licensee's auditors, regulators, test houses, advisers and customers, keeping
  the "modelled, not a certification" markings intact.
- **Q5** (issue 3). Rewrite the reverse-engineering restriction so it survives the
  mandatory exceptions of Directive 2009/24/EC under the chosen law.
- **Q6** (issue 8). Add an audit or usage-verification right and an anti-circumvention
  clause. Are they enforceable under the chosen law against a B2B licensee?

**Ownership and open source**

- **Q7** (issues 1, 6; facts in section 3). Given the contributor record and the
  current inbound terms, does Ashforde OÜ hold what it needs to license the engine,
  and Pro which embeds it, other than under the AGPL? Is the "by contributing, you
  agree" paragraph plus sign-off enough, or must a signed CLA be in place before the
  first external contribution is merged?
- **Q8** (issue 7). What third-party notices must ship with Pro binaries and with
  delivered dossiers, and in what form?
- **Q9** (issue 5). Replace the AGPL-based warranty wording in `legal/SUPPORT-SLA.md`
  section 4 with wording that fits a commercial licensee. Confirm the "modelled, not a
  certification" disclaimer creates no implied fitness or performance warranty under
  the chosen law.

**Money and risk**

- **Q10** (issue 9). Fix the payment mechanics: invoicing trigger, due date, late
  interest, suspension, taxes exclusive, and any withholding-tax clause for customers
  outside the European Union. Prices themselves stay founder-set.
- **Q11** (issue 10). Confirm the liability cap and carve-outs, and make the licence
  and the SLA say the same thing.
- **Q12** (issue 15). Can the standard terms be challenged as unfair standard terms
  under the chosen law even between businesses, and which clauses are most at risk?

**Compliance and continuity**

- **Q13** (issue 13). Draft the export-control and sanctions clause: licensee
  undertakings on end use, re-export and screening; Ashforde's right to suspend or
  refuse delivery; and how it interacts with the Pack B hold.
- **Q14** (issue 14). Should the Enterprise tier offer source-code escrow or a
  continuity commitment, and on what trigger events?

**Execution**

- **Q15** (issue 15). Approve the order-form wording produced by
  `commercial/tools/render.py` (parties, tier, term, fees table, payment details,
  governing-agreement sentence, signature blocks). Is a simple electronic signature
  enough, or should the parties use a qualified electronic signature (for example the
  Estonian identity card) under Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 (eIDAS)?
- **Q16**. Rename the document if "EULA" is wrong for B2B terms, and add a language
  clause.
