# Kshana × Baseband — Partnership Technical Brief

*Grounded in the Kshana codebase (public `kshana` v0.32.0) and arXiv:2607.06212, 2607.02566, 2606.22054 / 2606.24210. Every claim is marked **[BUILT]** (code exists), **[TESTED]** (checked against an external oracle/reference), or **[PLANNED/IDEA]** (not built). I use only public Kshana material and your own papers; nothing here uses or implies access to Baseband source, patents, or methods — only their anonymised outputs/test cases.*

## 1. Summary
Kshana is an open-core Rust simulator for GNSS/PNT **resilience and integrity**: it models clocks, propagation, spoofing/jamming, and integrity monitors, and validates them against published references. It already **[BUILT]** snapshot RAIM, several spoof detectors, a clock-aided detectability bound, and an I/Q acquisition front-end — the same "short-capture → fix" primitive Baseband productises. It does **not** implement OSNMA message-authentication verification; it only sizes the OSNMA/TESLA bit-budget **[BUILT]**. The complementary space is clear: Baseband owns *fast, low-power positioning*; Kshana owns *integrity/authentication analysis and validation tooling*. A low-risk first study — **can a few-ms snapshot receiver detect spoofing or support authentication, and at what energy/latency cost?** — is a genuine open research question and uses only anonymised data.

## 2. What Kshana does today (timing integrity & spoofing detection)
- **Snapshot RAIM** — single-epoch least-squares, chi-square fault detection, slope-based HPL/VPL. **[BUILT]** `src/raim.rs`. **[TESTED]** against an RTKLIB oracle, `tests/integrity_snapshot_raim_rtklib_oracle.rs`.
- **Spoof detectors** — AGC/power monitor, signal-quality (Early/Late) monitor, RAIM-parity consistency. **[BUILT]** `src/spoof_monitors.rs`.
- **Clock-aided spoof-detectability bound** — analytic minimum detectable time-error under holdover. **[BUILT]** `src/security.rs`, `src/clock_state.rs` (three-state clock). **[TESTED]** clock/stability path vs NIST SP1065 & allantools, `tests/allan_nist_sp1065_1000point.rs`.
- **Cross-sensor / cross-modality integrity** — min detectable spoof walk-rate via IMU/TRN cross-check; solution-separation across RF vs optical. **[BUILT]** `src/cross_sensor_integrity.rs`, `src/cross_raim.rs`.
- **I/Q acquisition front-end** — generate/acquire/track from short captures (the short-snapshot primitive). **[BUILT]** `src/iq/`. **[TESTED]** vs gnss-sdr and gps-sdr-sim oracles, `tests/gps_l1ca_gpssdrsim_cross_generator.rs`, `tests/lugre_acquisition_gnss_sdr_oracle.rs`.
- **Full ARAIM HPL/VPL** is referenced (`src/araim_reference.rs`) but production ARAIM is **[PLANNED/IDEA]** per `docs/INTEGRITY.md`.
- Your paper arXiv:2606.24210 (Conditional Timing Protection Level, calibrated on JammerTest 2024) and 2606.22054 (distribution-shift degradation of RF-impairment detectors) are the published backbone for the spoofing/holdover claims above.

## 3. OSNMA / navigation-message authentication
**Does Kshana implement or simulate it?** Only partially. Kshana **sizes** the OSNMA/TESLA bit-budget — 40-bit OSNMA field per 2 s E1-B I/NAV page (8-bit HKROOT + 32-bit MACK), 600 bits per 30 s subframe, TESLA key-disclosure latency, forgery probability 2^−tag_bits. **[BUILT]** `src/nma_budget.rs`; **[TESTED]** vs the Galileo OSNMA SIS-ICD (unit test `osnma_sis_icd_field_sizing_and_timing`). It does **not** verify MACs, roots, or the TESLA chain, and does not process a decoded nav message. So anything about *authenticating a snapshot* is **[PLANNED/IDEA]**.

**Is snapshot-OSNMA a sound open question?** Yes. OSNMA (and TESLA generally) is designed around *collecting the nav message over time* — MACs cover data pages and keys are disclosed in later subframes, so verification inherently spans tens of seconds. A receiver that wakes for a few ms cannot, on its own, see a full MACK/key sequence. The open questions are real and publishable: (a) can a snapshot receiver authenticate using **server-side assistance** (upload the captured snippet; a cloud service holds the OSNMA keys/chain and vouches for consistency)? (b) can partial OSNMA bits plus signal-level features give a *probabilistic* spoof/replay detector without full authentication? **Trade-offs:** full on-device OSNMA breaks the low-power duty-cycle model (seconds of RX vs ms); server-side assist shifts energy off-device but adds latency, connectivity dependence, and a privacy/data-exposure surface; partial/probabilistic detection is cheap but gives bounded assurance, not cryptographic proof. **Literature:** Galileo OSNMA SIS-ICD v1.1 (EUSPA); Fernández-Hernández et al., "A Navigation Message Authentication Proposal for the Galileo Open Service," *NAVIGATION* 2016; Perrig et al. (TESLA); Humphreys, "Detection Strategy for Cryptographic GNSS Anti-Spoofing," *IEEE TAES* 2013; Psiaki & Humphreys, "GNSS Spoofing and Detection," *Proc. IEEE* 2016.

## 4. Fit with Baseband
**Complementary, not competing.** Baseband sells *positioning* (a fix from ~2 ms on a Cortex-M4, RX off between fixes). Kshana sells *resilience/integrity analysis and validation* (does a fix survive spoofing/holdover; how to detect it; how to prove the monitor works). Kshana's I/Q front-end is a *simulation/validation* tool, not a production snapshot positioning engine, so it does not reproduce or compete with their patented fast-fix methods. **IP risk is low and manageable** if joint work: (i) uses only Baseband's *anonymised outputs/test cases* as inputs, never their source or method; (ii) treats their engine as a black box; (iii) keeps Kshana's integrity analysis on the output side. The one area to fence off explicitly is any snapshot *acquisition/positioning* technique, to avoid even the appearance of overlap with their patents.

OSNMA authentication itself is not yet implemented and would be part of the study."*

## 6. Concrete 2–6 week first project (anonymised data only)
**Title:** *Spoof/holdover detectability on snapshot fixes — a validation study.*
**Inputs (from Baseband, anonymised):** a set of their snapshot **position/time outputs** (and, if offered, consistency/quality flags) over labelled nominal, spoofed, and degraded test cases — outputs only, no source, no raw patented internals.
**What we do:** feed those outputs into Kshana's RAIM-parity, clock-aided detectability bound, and cross-sensor monitors; characterise the minimum detectable spoof/time-error vs their fix cadence and uncertainty; quantify the energy/latency cost of each detection strategy.
**Output:** a short report + reproducible Kshana scenario/notebook giving, per test case, detectability vs cost curves and a recommended monitor configuration.
**Effort:** ~3–4 weeks; **[PLANNED/IDEA]** (uses existing **[BUILT]** monitors, no new patented IP).

## 7. Commercial licence — what it must cover
Because Baseband ships closed IP *embedded inside other companies' products*, a Kshana commercial licence (Kshana is AGPL-3.0 + commercial dual-licensed) must grant, beyond the plain AGPL terms:
- **Proprietary/closed-source distribution** — a waiver of AGPL §13 (network/source-disclosure) and copyleft, so Kshana-derived code can ship inside closed products.
- **Redistribution & sublicensing to their customers/OEMs**, including embedding in firmware delivered to third parties.
- **Field-of-use and per-product/royalty terms** as negotiated, plus clear ownership of improvements and of any joint work (default: each keeps its background IP; foreground split defined).
- **Warranty/indemnity and support/maintenance** scope, and an explicit statement that the licence covers only Kshana code — not any Baseband patent — keeping the two IP estates cleanly separated.
