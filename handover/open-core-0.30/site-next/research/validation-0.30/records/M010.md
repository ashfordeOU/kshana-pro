### Claim as checked (narrowed in writing now, before the run)

- Observable-level monitors only (the plan's scope): the pseudorange RAIM consistency test
  (`spoof_monitors::parity_raim_test`) and a clock-aided monitor whose decision bound is
  `security::min_detectable_offset_ns` (k = `security::SPOOF_DETECT_K` = 5). The fused FoM, AGC and
  SQM layers are outside the claim (they need IF data).
- GPS signals only: the monitors run on GPS L1 C/A + L2C pseudoranges, so sub-scenarios that spoof
  only Galileo (2.1.3, 2.3.8) are outside the claim.
- Stationary receivers only (the dynamic-rover folders are outside the claim).

### Windows (fixed now)

- cal_start = max(recording start, previous slot end, onset - 240 s). Calibration
  [cal_start, cal_start + 60 s); pre-onset false-alarm window [cal_start + 60 s, onset), at least
  15 s long or the onset is not evaluable; detection window [onset, onset + 10 s]; the slice runs to
  min(slot end, onset + 300 s) for M011.
- One epoch per integer GPS second (the first 5 Hz sample of each second).

### Processing (fixed now)

- GPS satellites with both `pseudorange_L1` (written as C1C) and `pseudorange_L2` (C2L) only; the
  ionosphere-free combination through `pvt::assemble_epoch(dual_freq = true)`, elevation mask 10 deg,
  a-priori position = the receiver's first `nav_pvt` fix of the recording, default meteo;
  `pvt::solve_spp`.
- RAIM: `parity_raim_test` on the post-fit residuals and line-of-sight rows at the fix, sigma = 3.0 m,
  p_fa = 1e-5 per epoch. An epoch with at least 5 satellites whose solve fails is an alarm.
- Clock monitor: b_n = clock_bias_m / c. A step between consecutive epochs within 100 m (/c) of a
  non-zero integer number of milliseconds is a receiver clock reset and is removed. Calibration:
  r = variance of the residuals of a least-squares line (bias + drift) through the calibration
  clock samples; q_wf = ADEV(1 s)^2 and q_rw = 3 (ADEV(10 s) / sqrt(10/3))^2 of the calibration phase
  (`allan::overlapping_adev`); sigma_mon = `security::monitor_sigma_s(q_wf, q_rw, r, 1, 1)`.
  Prediction: least-squares line through the trailing (up to) 60 non-alarmed epochs (at least 10),
  extrapolated to the epoch; alarm when |b_n - prediction| > k sigma_mon with k = 5, which is
  `min_detectable_offset_ns(q_wf, q_rw, r, 1, 1, 5)`.
- Detection = first alarm of either monitor at or after the onset. Pre-onset alarms are counted
  (and fail the row) but do not latch.

Verdict rule: PROMOTE only if all 8 evaluable onsets are detected in [onset, onset + 10 s] and none
of the 8 pre-onset windows has an alarm.

Known risk, stated before the run: the published onsets are scheduled slot starts at one-minute
resolution, and a power-ramped spoofer captures the receiver some time after it starts radiating,
so a capture later than 10 s after the slot start will fail the tolerance even if the monitor works.

Findings (for the row's oracle text and the CHANGELOG):

1. **The published onsets are schedule slot starts, not RF onsets.** Where the pre-onset window was
   clean (5 onsets), the first observable effect on GPS L1 C/A + L2C came 10.2 s to 39 s after the
   published slot start, and in every case as a loss of dual-frequency GPS tracking (the
   solve-failure alarm, or for 2.3.10 a clock jump), not as a RAIM inconsistency. In 2.1.1 (power
   ramp) the RAIM statistic stayed at 0-2 against a threshold of 26 for the full 300 s after the
   published onset: no capture was visible in that horizon. A 10 s tolerance against minute-resolution
   schedule times was not achievable on this dataset by any observable-level monitor.
2. **The clock-aided monitor false-alarms on the receiver's TCXO.** With the bound taken from the
   calibration's 1 s noise and ADEV(10 s) (`security::monitor_sigma_s`, q_drift = 0), the 5-sigma bound
   is 11-17 ns, while the u-blox TCXO's frequency wander (drift changing from about 22 to 26 ns/s over
   five minutes in 2.1.1) makes the 60 s trailing-line prediction error grow past it about 80 s after
   calibration. Because the pre-registered predictor excludes alarmed epochs, the first false alarm
   then latches (the prediction freezes and the innovation grows without limit). This is a property
   of the monitor as specified for this test (and of the security floor's assumption that the clock's
   red noise is known), not a code defect in `src/`; no `src/` file was changed.
3. **2.3.12's pre-onset window was not clean.** RAIM statistics of 373-576 (threshold 33) in the
   window after the published end of 2.3.15 (17:05 local) show the receiver still spoofed; the
   published slot end is not the end of the attack.
4. Not evaluable (as pre-registered): the stationary 2.6.1 and "2.6.3" recordings. Out of scope:
   Galileo-only sub-scenarios 2.1.3 and 2.3.8, and the dynamic-rover folders.
