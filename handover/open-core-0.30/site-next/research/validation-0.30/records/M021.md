## Pre-registration (written 2026-10-01T03:25Z, before any comparison was run)

Oracle kind: **Library**. Basilisk 2.9.1 (ISC, https://github.com/AVSLab/basilisk, the `bsk` wheel in
`~/Code/kshana-oracles/.venv`) `GravityGradientEffector` attached to a `spacecraft.Spacecraft` hub,
with a point-mass Earth whose mu is set to Kshana's 3.986004418e14. The torque is read from the
effector evaluated at the initial state (no propagation).

Claim as checked (narrowed in writing now): the gravity-gradient disturbance torque
`gravity_gradient_torque_max(h, dI) = (3/2)(mu/R^3)|I_max - I_min|` as the peak over attitude. The
RSS pointing budget is the quadrature sum of caller-supplied 1-sigma numbers, a design aggregation
with no external truth, so it is out of the validated claim. Kshana emits no torque vector, so the
plan's "torque vector" comparison becomes the magnitude comparisons below.

Cases: altitudes {300, 400, 600, 800, 1200} km; inertia tensors: 3 diagonal (principal) and 5 general
non-diagonal tensors (random symmetric positive-definite, seed 20261001, built as R diag(I1,I2,I3) R^T
with random rotations); dI = max eigenvalue - min eigenvalue (numpy `eigh` in the generator).

Comparisons and tolerances (fixed now):
1. **Peak (the 45 deg attitude).** Basilisk |torque| with the nadir direction in the body frame at
   45 deg between the minimum- and maximum-inertia principal axes equals Kshana's peak within
   **1e-12 relative**.
2. **Upper bound.** For 2000 random attitudes per (altitude, tensor), Basilisk |torque| is at most
   Kshana's peak times (1 + 1e-12).
