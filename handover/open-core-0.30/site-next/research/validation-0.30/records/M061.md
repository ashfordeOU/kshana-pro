## Pre-registration (written 2026-10-01T03:30Z, before any comparison was run)

Oracle kind: **Library**. NaveGo v1.4 (git tag `v1.4`, commit 24d9488), LGPL-3.0, run as a tool
under GNU Octave 11.1.0: its strapdown mechanization (`ins/att_update.m` quaternion mode,
`vel_update.m`, `pos_update.m`, `earth_rate.m`, `transport_rate.m`, `gravity.m`, `radius.m`), its
IMU signal generators (`simulation/acc_gen.m`, `gyro_gen.m`) and its unit conversion
(`conversions/imu_si_errors.m`). Free-inertial: no aiding at all. https://github.com/rodralez/NaveGo

What is compared: each per-term law of `inertial::coast::CoastModel` (`Contribution::error_m(t)`)
against NaveGo's free-inertial position error produced by that error source alone.

IMU error profile (fixed now): Kshana's `ImuGrade::Tactical` coefficients, given to NaveGo in
datasheet units and converted by NaveGo's own code and constants: accelerometer bias 300 ug
(x 1e-6 x NaveGo G = 9.80665), VRW 0.06 m/s/sqrt(h) (NaveGo `imu_si_errors`, a_std = vrw/60/sqrt(dt)),
scale factor 300 ppm, gyro bias 1 deg/h (x pi/180/3600), ARW 0.05 deg/sqrt(h) (NaveGo `imu_si_errors`,
g_std = arw/60 x pi/180 / sqrt(dt)).

Platform (fixed now): latitude 45 deg, longitude 0, height 1000 m, level, heading north; IMU at
10 Hz; exact initial state. The error-free IMU signal comes from NaveGo `acc_gen`/`gyro_gen` with
every error set to zero (they add gravity, Coriolis and Earth rate themselves).

Terms and scenarios (fixed now):
- T1 `accel_bias`: stationary; constant +b_a on the x accelerometer. One deterministic run.
- T2 `gyro_bias_tilt`: stationary; constant +b_g on the x gyro. One deterministic run.
- T3 `scale_factor_cruise`: from rest, kinematic x acceleration 10 m/s^2 for the first 1.0 s, then
  cruise north at 10 m/s; the x specific force multiplied by (1 + s). Kshana:
  `CoastModel::new(tactical, speed 10, accel 0, ..)`, term `s v t`. One deterministic run.
- T4 `scale_factor_accel`: from rest, sustained kinematic x acceleration 0.01 m/s^2 for the whole
  run; x specific force multiplied by (1 + s). Kshana: `CoastModel::new(tactical, speed 0,
  accel 0.01, ..)`, term `s a t^2 / 2`. One deterministic run.
- T5 `velocity_random_walk`: stationary; NaveGo white noise of a_std on the x AND y accelerometers;
  300 seeds (Octave `randn("seed", k)`, k = 1..300).
- T6 `angle_random_walk`: stationary; NaveGo white noise of g_std on the x AND y gyros; 300 seeds.

Deterministic terms are run once at their 1-sigma coefficient: a Monte Carlo over random bias draws
would add only sampling noise to the same claim (the law is linear in the coefficient).

Error (fixed now): run minus the error-free run on the same trajectory, north = dlat (R_M + h),
east = dlon (R_N + h) cos(lat) with NaveGo `radius()`. Deterministic terms: horizontal magnitude
sqrt(dN^2 + dE^2). Stochastic terms (noise on two axes): per-axis RMS
sqrt(mean over seeds of (dN^2 + dE^2) / 2), which is the one-axis quantity the law states.

Coast durations (fixed now): G = {30, 60, 120, 300, 600, 1200, 1800, 3600} s, from half a minute to
the one-hour coast the module's own tests reason about.

Tolerance (the plan's, fixed now): |NaveGo - Kshana| <= 0.05 x NaveGo at every duration of G, for
every one of the six terms (48 comparisons). PROMOTE only if all 48 hold.

Statistical note, stated before the run: with 300 seeds on two axes (600 one-axis samples) the
relative sampling standard deviation of an RMS is about 1/sqrt(2 x 600) = 2.9 %, so the 5 % bar
on T5 and T6 is about 1.7 sigma of the oracle's own sampling error. The plan's seed count and
tolerance are kept as written.

error tables, `NOTICE.md`). Test:
`tests/ins_coast_navego_montecarlo_oracle.rs::coast_error_terms_match_the_navego_monte_carlo`.

### Pre-run refinement (2026-10-01T03:45Z, still before any comparison was run)

Reading NaveGo's `simulation/gyro_gen.m` showed it does not add the Earth rate to a supplied body
rate (its `g_err_b` term multiplies the supplied rate by the skew of omega_in), so a stationary
gyro signal from it would be zero and the free-inertial attitude would drift at the Earth rate.
The error-free signal is therefore formed directly from the NaveGo functions the mechanization
itself uses: specific force by `acc_gen`'s formula (kinematic acceleration + `coriolis()` -
`gravity()`), angular rate omega_ib^b = `earth_rate()` + `transport_rate()` for the NED-locked body.
White noise is drawn by `acc_gen`/`gyro_gen`'s formula (std x `randn`) with NaveGo's
`imu_si_errors` standard deviations. Nothing about the terms, durations, statistic or tolerance
changed.

Finding (for the row's oracle text and CHANGELOG "Findings"): the coast model's growth laws are
flat-Earth monomials with no Schuler feedback. Against an independent full strapdown
mechanization (NaveGo v1.4, LGPL-3.0, under Octave) every law agrees within 5 % up to 600 s
(about one eighth of the 84.4-minute Schuler period), but beyond that the accelerometer-bias,
gyro-bias, VRW and ARW laws overstate the free-inertial horizontal error: by 7 to 21 % at 1200 s,
19 to 55 % at 1800 s and 2.2x to 8x at one hour. Crossings the model reports beyond about 10
minutes (and the TRN "tactical hour" reasoning) are therefore pessimistic for a Schuler-tuned
navigator. Likely remedy, for an owner decision: either state the domain (coasts up to about
600 s) in the capability, or replace the t^2/t^3/t^1.5/t^2.5 laws by their Schuler-loop forms
(e.g. b_a (1 - cos w_s t) / w_s^2). The 5 % agreement up to 600 s was observed, not pre-registered,
so it cannot promote a narrowed claim in this batch; a narrowed claim needs a new pre-registered
comparison.

Unexplained observation, flagged for follow-up (does not affect the verdict): the two moving
scale-factor runs (T3, T4) follow the flat-Earth law to within 1.5 % even at 3600 s, with no visible
Schuler bounding, while the four stationary terms show it clearly. Either NaveGo's coupling of a
velocity error built during motion differs from the stationary case, or the harness's error-free
reference absorbs it; I did not resolve which.

sample 1), so the cruise speed reached 9 m/s and T3 read 12 to 14 % low at every duration. The
generator was corrected and T3 re-run after that result had been seen; the corrected rows are

Statistics: the T5/T6 differences at 30 to 120 s (-1.3 to -3.0 %) are inside the 2.9 % sampling
standard deviation of a 600-sample RMS stated before the run.
