Geometry

Which satellites can you see, and how good is their geometry?

Pick a place, a time and a mix of global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) and low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites, draw the skyline that blocks your view, and see the dilution of precision (DOP) and the position error it implies. Every number comes from the Kshana engine running in this browser. What this shows.

Advisory software. Kshana is not type-approved navigation equipment (IEC 61108, IEC 61162) and has not been assessed against any performance standard. The figures here are MODELLED from nominal constellations, not from the real sky on the date shown, and are aids to judgement. The operator stays responsible for the safe navigation of the vessel or vehicle and for any decision to rely on, or stop relying on, a fix. A low DOP is not thereby a good fix: it says nothing about interference, spoofing, multipath or a faulty satellite.

      What this shows

      • The skyplot is the sky seen from the place: each mark is a satellite at its azimuth (compass bearing) and elevation (angle above the horizon). Colour and shape both say which constellation it belongs to.
      • Dilution of precision (DOP) is how much the satellites' geometry multiplies the error of each range measurement. Satellites spread across the sky give low DOP; satellites bunched together, or all high overhead, give high DOP. GDOP is the geometric DOP (position and time), PDOP the three-dimensional position DOP, HDOP horizontal, VDOP vertical and TDOP the receiver clock (time) DOP.
      • Position error ≈ σUERE × DOP. The user-equivalent range error (σUERE) is the one-sigma error of each range: satellite clock and orbit, the atmosphere, multipath and receiver noise together. Multiplying it by HDOP gives the one-sigma horizontal error, and by VDOP the vertical.
      • The error ellipse is the one-sigma horizontal error from the geometry's covariance: longer along the directions the satellites constrain least. The Monte Carlo cloud is many seeded random range errors pushed through the same geometry; its spread matches σUERE × DOP, and the same seed always gives the same cloud.
      • The skyline is what blocks the sky: buildings, quays, cranes or mountain sides. A satellite behind it is not used. The presets are illustrative outlines, not surveys of any real place; draw your own on the skyplot.
      • What it leaves out. Nominal constellations (not the real satellites on that date or their health), no signal strength or multipath from the skyline, and no interference. The model is MODELLED, not validated against a receiver at the place.
      • The engine runs here as WebAssembly. Nothing is fetched from another site and nothing you set is sent anywhere.
