Rehearse the minute GNSS goes dark.

Kshana is an open-source simulator for PNT (positioning, navigation and timing) resilience. Replay jamming, spoofing and clock holdover when GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) signals fail, in a Rust engine that runs right here in your browser, and see which results were checked against independent references.

A spoofer walks the solution off its true track. Kshana's spoofing and meaconing detectors flag the divergence.

With GNSS gone, the clock is on its own. Kshana predicts how long it stays inside its guard interval.

    Clocks and timing

    The clock ladder.

    Every clock the engine carries, from a receiver's crystal to a space optical master, on one Allan-deviation chart. Then lose GNSS for a few hours and watch each clock's time error against your guard.

    σy(τ), Allan deviation, log–logDatasheet points and class figures from the engine source

    Time error here is σy(τ)·τ, the convention the engine's lunar clock module uses, without ageing or temperature terms: an order-of-magnitude comparison, labelled Illustrative. The engine's holdover and telecom kinds model the full noise, ageing and temperature budget.

    Validated0.84–1.03

    Holdover predicted ÷ measured on a real caesium record held out from fitting, inside the pre-set factor of 1.5.

    ValidatedNIST SP 1065

    The Allan-deviation code reproduces the reference values of NIST Special Publication 1065.

    The timing wall

    Three ways time fails a system, each backed by a bundled scenario.

    MTIE, maximum time interval error, against an ITU-T mask
    PASS

    The mask is ITU-T G.8272 Table 1 or 2, primary reference time clock class A or B, as the engine carries it. The MTIE curve is Illustrative; telecom-prtc-holdover-24h and telecom-tie-ingest compute real MTIE and TDEV (time deviation) with a pass, a fail and a margin per mask.

    Slot timingIllustrative

    How long an OCXO on a low-Earth-orbit smallsat stays inside a routing slot's guard after its last GNSS fix, and how often it needs a fix. slot-timing-ocxo-leo

    Lunar timeModelled
    ≈ 57µs per day

    A clock on the lunar surface ticks faster than one on Earth's geoid: the engine sums the self-potential and second-order Doppler terms and reports the rate beside the published 56 to 59 µs/day band. Modelled, not certified. lunar-time-offset · lunar-time-budget

    Kshana is not an RF signal simulator and not a GNSS receiver. It is a study and trade-off instrument: it simulates what jamming, spoofing and signal loss do to a navigation and timing solution, and tells you which of its answers are validated. Not a flight product.
