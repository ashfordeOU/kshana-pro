L-band console · the spectrum kind

Watch a jammer take the band.

The GNSS L band as one PSD (power spectral density): frequency across, time down, colour for power above the noise floor. The signals sit under that floor, so what lights up is the jammers: a chirp, a CW (continuous-wave) tone and narrowband noise. Hover, tap or use the arrow keys for each band's C/N0 (carrier-to-noise density) and J/S (jammer-to-signal ratio).

      04Scroll story · one chained run

      From a clean sky to a free-running clock.

      One receiver, {{CAMP_DUR}} of mission time: a clean sky, a jammer, a spoofer that moves the answer instead of blocking it, then a clock left on its own. The {{CONST_N}} satellites are the four systems of the constellation run, on the real Earth. Scroll.

      01 · Nominal

      Every link, accounted for.

      {{S_TRK}} satellites tracked at a mean C/N0 (carrier-to-noise density) of {{S_CN0}} dB-Hz. The vertical protection level stays between {{S_PL0}} and {{S_PL1}} m, under the {{S_AL}} m alert limit.

      02 · Jamming

      Noise swallows the sky.

      A broadband jammer {{S_JAMKM}} km away comes on at {{S_JAM1}} dBW. C/N0 falls to {{S_CN0J}} dB-Hz, just above the {{S_FLOOR}} dB-Hz tracking floor. Loud, so the receiver knows.

      03 · Spoofing

      A lie that looks like a fix.

      A spoofer pushes the clock at {{S_RATE}} ns/s. The RF (radio-frequency) detector alarms at {{S_RF_T}}; the clock-aided monitor needs {{S_DET_S}} s, and by then {{S_OFF}} ns are already pulled in.

      04 · Holdover

      Then time starts to drift.

      The jammer goes to {{S_JAM2}} dBW and every satellite is lost. The clock free-runs from the {{S_CARRY}} ns the spoofer left and crosses its {{S_GUARD}} ns guard at {{S_GUARD_T}}; the inertial error reaches {{S_INS_KM}} km.
